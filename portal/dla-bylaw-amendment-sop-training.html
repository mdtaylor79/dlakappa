Module 1 of 7

Overview & Constitutional Authority

Understand what this SOP governs, who it applies to, and the constitutional foundation that gives the Denton-Lewisville Chapter the authority to adopt and amend its own bylaws.

Module Objectives

  • Cite the KAP constitutional provision that authorizes alumni chapter bylaws
  • Explain the non-conflict standard and what it requires
  • State the filing requirements (or lack thereof) for alumni chapter bylaw amendments
  • Identify all parties responsible for following this SOP

What This SOP Governs

This Standard Operating Procedure governs the complete lifecycle of a bylaw amendment proposal for the Denton-Lewisville (TX) Alumni Chapter — from a member’s initial research through portal submission, committee review, membership vote, and official adoption. It is not a suggestion; all parties are responsible for following this procedure to ensure every amendment is constitutionally sound, properly deliberated, and permanently recorded.

This SOP was prepared by the DLA Bylaws Committee and derives its authority from the KAP Constitution and Statutes 2025–27 (87th Grand Chapter edition).

Constitutional Authority

KAP Constitution Art. XXX, Sec. 4
Alumni chapters shall have the power to adopt bylaws for their governance, provided that no such bylaws shall be in conflict with the Constitution, Statutes, Ritual, or other enactment of Kappa Alpha Psi.

This provision does two things simultaneously:

  1. It grants the Denton-Lewisville Chapter the power to adopt and amend its own governing bylaws without seeking approval from IHQ or the Province.
  2. It constrains that power with a non-conflict standard — no chapter bylaw may contradict, supersede, or be inconsistent with any national governing document.

The Non-Conflict Standard

The non-conflict standard is the fundamental legal test every proposed bylaw amendment must pass. Before any proposal advances, it must be verified against four national governing documents:

National DocumentWhat It GovernsAmendment Impact
KAP ConstitutionFundamental structure, authority, and rights of the fraternity and its chaptersChapter bylaws cannot conflict with constitutional provisions
KAP StatutesDetailed operational rules, procedures, and requirementsChapter bylaws cannot override or contradict statutory requirements
KAP RitualCeremonial content and proceduresChapter bylaws cannot alter or conflict with ritual requirements
Other Enactments of KAPGrand Chapter resolutions, orders, and official directivesChapter bylaws cannot contradict official Grand Chapter orders

Filing Requirements

Important: KAP Art. XXX, Sec. 4 prescribes no filing obligation to IHQ or the Province Polemarch for alumni chapter bylaw amendments. Internal compliance responsibility rests entirely with the Bylaws Committee and the Keeper of Records. The chapter governs itself on this matter.

This means the chapter’s adopted bylaws and amendments do not need to be submitted to, approved by, or filed with IHQ or the Province. However, they must still conform to national law.

Parties Responsible Under This SOP

PartyPrimary Responsibility
Any Financial MemberMay initiate a proposal; responsible for research and complete submission
Keeper of Records (KOR)Receipt, logging, date-stamping, official recordkeeping, and archiving
Bylaws CommitteeConstitutional review, classification, priority determination, and text update
Bylaws Committee ChairmanCo-signs all adopted amendments; oversees compliance certification

Phase 1 — Member Preparation

Before submitting anything, the proposing member must complete three preparation steps to research and map constitutional alignment. This phase cannot be skipped — incomplete or unconstitutional proposals will be rejected.

Module Objectives

  • Complete the three-step member preparation process in the correct sequence
  • Locate the current DLA Bylaws and the KAP Constitution & Statutes 2025–27
  • Apply the three-part alignment framework to a proposed change
  • Use the cross-reference document to map existing bylaw sections

Why Preparation Matters

The most common reason proposals are rejected is insufficient pre-submission research. A member who submits without reviewing national law often discovers — after the committee review — that the proposal conflicts with a constitutional provision or duplicates existing language. This wastes committee time and delays the process for everyone. Phase 1 is designed to prevent these failures before they happen.

Step 1: Review the Current DLA Bylaws

  1. Locate the current version on the DLA member portal. Do not use an old printed copy or an unofficial version — only the current portal version is authoritative.
  2. Read the full article and section(s) that your proposal will address. Do not rely on a summary or another brother’s paraphrase — read the actual text.
  3. Identify exactly what the current language says — or document a gap if no language currently exists for your topic. Both existing language and gaps are valid starting points for a proposal.

Step 2: Review the KAP Constitution & Statutes 2025–27

Access the Constitution & Statutes of Kappa Alpha Psi® 2025–27 (87th Grand Chapter edition) from the member portal. Find the provisions relevant to your proposal area and determine which of three relationships applies:

National Law PositionWhat It Means for Your Proposal
Directly mandates somethingYour proposed language must align with — and may not contradict — the national mandate
Expressly prohibits somethingYour proposal cannot include the prohibited element under any circumstance
Is silent on the matterThe chapter has full discretion under Art. XXX, Sec. 4 to adopt whatever language serves the chapter

Step 3: Map Alignment or Conflict

Document the relationship between your proposal and national law using the three-part alignment framework below. This documentation must be included in your portal submission under the Constitutional / Statutory Alignment field.

Alignment Framework

  • ALIGNS WITH: The proposal is consistent with or directly required by the Constitution/Statutes. Cite the specific provision (e.g., “Art. XV, Sec. 3 requires annual financial audits; this amendment implements that requirement at the chapter level”).
  • CONFLICTS WITH: The current bylaw or your proposed language contradicts a national provision. A submission with this determination will be rejected unless the conflict is resolved before submission.
  • WITHIN CHAPTER DISCRETION: National law is silent on this matter. The chapter may adopt language per Art. XXX, Sec. 4. Use “Art. XXX, Sec. 4 — chapter discretion” as your citation.
Resource: The Proposed Bylaw Amendments cross-reference document (DLA Bylaws Committee, August 2026) maps every current DLA bylaw section against constitutional and statutory citations. Use it as a guide before completing your alignment documentation.

Phase 2 — Portal Submission

After completing Phase 1 research, the member submits the official proposal through the DLA member portal. All 8 required fields must be completed — incomplete submissions are returned without review.

Module Objectives

  • Identify who is eligible to submit a bylaw amendment proposal
  • Navigate to the correct portal submission form
  • Complete all 8 required fields accurately
  • Describe what happens immediately after a successful submission

Who May Submit

Only financial members in good standing may submit bylaw amendment proposals. Members with administrative holds, unpaid dues, or other standing issues are not eligible until their status is resolved.

Step 4: Access the Member Portal Form

Submission URL: https://dlakappa.org/portal/bylaws-amendment-form.html
Log in with your chapter member credentials before accessing the form.

Step 5: Complete All 8 Required Fields

All fields are mandatory. Submitting with any field incomplete will result in the proposal being returned. The table below details each required field and what it must contain.

#Field NameWhat to Provide
1Article / SectionWhich article and section of the current bylaws the proposal addresses
2Current VerbiageThe exact existing text — or identify the gap if no current language exists
3Proposed VerbiageThe exact replacement or new language being proposed
4Rationale for ChangeWhy the change is needed; cite any constitutional or statutory basis
5PrioritySubmitter’s suggested urgency: Critical / High / Medium / Low
6Type of ChangeAddition · Amendment · Deletion · Correction
7Constitutional / Statutory AlignmentCite the specific KAP Constitution or Statute provision supporting the proposal
8Expected ImpactHow chapter operations, governance, or compliance will improve
Field 7 Requirement: You must provide a constitutional or statutory citation. If national law is silent, use “Art. XXX, Sec. 4 — chapter discretion” as your citation. Do not leave this field blank or write “N/A.”

Step 6: Submit the Proposal

  1. Review all fields for accuracy and completeness before clicking Submit.
  2. Upon submission, you will receive an automated confirmation email containing a unique tracking number.
  3. Retain the tracking number — it is your reference for all future correspondence on this proposal. You will need it to follow up on status or respond to committee inquiries.

Phase 3 — KOR & Bylaws Committee Review

Once submitted, the proposal enters a structured review process: the Keeper of Records logs it, and the Bylaws Committee verifies constitutional compliance, confirms priority, and assigns a classification that determines the voting path.

Module Objectives

  • Describe the KOR’s receipt and logging responsibilities and timeline
  • List the four checks performed in the constitutional conflict review
  • Explain how the committee verifies and may adjust priority
  • Distinguish between Editorial and Substantive classifications and their voting paths

Step 7: KOR Receipt & Logging

The Keeper of Records (KOR) is the first official recipient of every portal submission. The KOR must:

  1. Receive the portal submission and log it in the official Amendment Tracking Register within 3 business days of submission.
  2. Assign an internal receipt number and record the submission date. This creates the official timestamp for the amendment’s lifecycle.
  3. Forward the proposal to the Bylaws Committee Chair with the receipt number and submission date.

Step 8: Constitutional Conflict Check

The Bylaws Committee reviews the proposal against the KAP Constitution & Statutes 2025–27. The committee checks for all four of the following:

Four-Point Conflict Check

  • Direct conflict with any constitutional or statutory provision
  • Misalignment with the non-conflict standard (Art. XXX, Sec. 4)
  • Duplication of existing bylaw language that already addresses the issue
  • Internal contradiction with other current DLA bylaw provisions

If any of the four issues is found, the proposal is flagged for rejection. If none are found, the proposal advances to priority verification.

Step 9: Priority Verification

The committee reviews the submitter’s suggested priority level and may adjust it. Adjustments are based on:

  • Severity of the constitutional misalignment or governance gap being addressed
  • Legal or disciplinary exposure risk to the chapter
  • Operational impact on chapter functions
The committee’s determination of priority is final and will be noted in the acceptance/rejection correspondence sent to the submitting member.
Priority LevelGeneral Meaning
CriticalConstitutional violation or legal exposure requiring immediate resolution
HighSignificant governance gap or operational risk affecting chapter functions
MediumImprovement to clarity or efficiency without immediate risk
LowMinor correction, clarification, or editorial refinement

Step 10: Process Classification

Based on the nature of the change, the committee assigns one of two classifications. This classification determines the voting path the proposal will follow:

EDITORIAL

Applies to: Spelling, grammar, cross-references, typographical errors — no change to substance, rights, or duties

Vote path: Board of Directors only — 2/3 affirmative vote

No membership notice required; no two-reading process

SUBSTANTIVE

Applies to: Any change to a right, duty, procedure, qualification, officer structure, or financial obligation

Vote path: Full membership — two-reading process, 2/3 affirmative vote of financial members

21-day advance notice to all members required

Phase 4 — Accept or Reject Decision

After completing the four-point review, the Bylaws Committee Chair makes a formal determination on each submission. This phase has strict timelines and specific requirements for the correspondence sent to the submitting member.

Module Objectives

  • Identify all conditions that require a proposal to be rejected
  • Identify all conditions that must be met for a proposal to be accepted
  • State the timeline for decision correspondence
  • Describe the required contents of both rejection and acceptance correspondence

Step 11: Decision — Does the Proposal Advance?

The Bylaws Committee Chair, in consultation with the committee, makes a formal determination on each submission. The decision is either rejection or acceptance. Both outcomes require written correspondence to the submitting member within 10 business days.

Rejection — Conditions & Required Correspondence

A proposal is rejected if it meets any one of the following four conditions:

  • Duplicates existing bylaw language already in effect
  • Contradicts or conflicts with another current DLA bylaw provision
  • Directly conflicts with or is misaligned with the KAP Constitution or Statutes
  • Submission is incomplete (one or more of the 8 required fields is missing)

Required Contents of Rejection Correspondence

  • The specific rejection reason(s) — not a general statement
  • The constitutional or bylaw provision that creates the conflict
  • Advice on whether the proposal may be revised and resubmitted

Acceptance — Conditions & Required Correspondence

A proposal is accepted when all four of the following conditions are met:

  • No constitutional or statutory conflict is identified
  • No internal contradiction with existing DLA bylaws exists
  • The proposal addresses a genuine gap, correction, or improvement
  • All 8 required fields are properly completed

Required Contents of Acceptance Correspondence

  • Confirmation of acceptance and the final tracking number
  • The committee’s priority determination (which may differ from the member’s suggestion)
  • The classification: Editorial or Substantive
  • The anticipated vote schedule

Module 6 of 7

Phase 5 — Voting Process

Accepted proposals follow one of two voting paths based on classification. Editorial amendments go to the Board of Directors only. Substantive amendments require a full two-reading membership process with 21-day advance notice.

Module Objectives

  • Apply the correct voting path for Editorial vs. Substantive classifications
  • Describe the 21-day advance notice requirement for substantive amendments
  • Explain the two-reading process and when a vote is taken
  • Identify when a secret ballot is required
  • Describe the compliance certification required before the final vote is certified

Editorial Path

A1
Place on Board Agenda

The Bylaws Committee Chair notifies the Chapter Polemarch and Secretary. The proposal is added to the Board of Directors agenda for the next regularly scheduled meeting. No advance notice period or reading requirements apply.

A2
Board of Directors Vote

The amendment is presented and voted on by the Board of Directors. Adoption requires a 2/3 affirmative vote of Board members present and voting.

A3
Outcome

PASSED → Proceed to Phase 6 — Adoption & Filing.
FAILED → Proposal is closed. The submitting member is notified and may revise and resubmit.

Substantive Path

B1
21-Day Advance Notice

The Bylaws Committee issues written notice to the full chapter membership at least 21 days before the First Reading. The notice must include the full proposed text and the constitutional citation.

B2
First Reading

The amendment is formally introduced and read aloud at a regular chapter meeting. Discussion may occur, but no vote is taken at this meeting. The proposal is tabled to the next meeting.

B3
Second Reading & Vote

At the following regular meeting, the amendment is read a second time. After deliberation, a vote is called. Adoption requires a 2/3 affirmative vote of financial members present and voting. A secret ballot is required if any member requests it.

B4
Bylaws Committee Compliance Certification

Prior to the final vote being certified, the Bylaws Committee Chair certifies in writing that the adopted language is compliant with KAP Art. XXX, Sec. 4. This certification must occur before the vote result is official.

B5
Outcome

PASSED → Proceed to Phase 6 — Adoption & Filing.
FAILED → Proposal is closed. The submitting member is notified and may revise and resubmit.

Phase 6 — Adoption, Filing & Archiving

A passed amendment is not complete until it is officially named, integrated into the master bylaws, dual-signed, stamped by the KOR, communicated to the chapter, and properly archived. All six steps are required.

Module Objectives

  • Apply the chapter’s amendment naming convention to assign a reference ID
  • Describe the process for updating the master bylaws document
  • Identify who must co-sign adopted amendment pages and why
  • Explain the KOR’s role as sole authority for the official bylaws version
  • State the timeline for posting the updated document to the member portal
  • Describe the archiving convention for superseded bylaws versions

The Six Post-Passage Steps

StepActionResponsible Party
AAmendment Naming: Assign reference ID (e.g., DLA-2026-A01). Log the ID, article/section, date adopted, and vote tally in the Amendment Log.Bylaws Committee
BUpdate Bylaws Document: Incorporate adopted language into the master bylaws. Accept all tracked changes; reconcile formatting with existing document style.Bylaws Committee
CDual Signature Authentication: Sign the updated pages bearing the amendment. Two signatures required: (1) Bylaws Committee Chairman, (2) Chapter Polemarch.Chairman & Polemarch
DKOR File & Stamp: Apply a file date stamp to signed pages. Record the amendment in the official registry. The KOR is the sole authority for the official version.Keeper of Records
EChapter Notification: At the next regular meeting, formally inform the chapter body, citing the amendment ID and effective date. Post the updated document to the member portal within 5 business days.Keeper of Records
FArchive Prior Version: Rename the superseded version per archiving convention (e.g., DLA_Bylaws_v2025-09_SUPERSEDED.pdf). Store in the official records archive. The current version is the only active governing document.Bylaws Committee / KOR

Amendment Naming Convention

Every adopted amendment receives a reference ID in the following format:

DLA-[YEAR]-A[##]
Example: DLA-2026-A01 — first amendment adopted in 2026
Example: DLA-2026-A02 — second amendment adopted in 2026

The reference ID, along with the article/section, adoption date, and vote tally, is recorded in the Amendment Log maintained by the Bylaws Committee.

Dual Signature Authentication

The adopted amendment pages must bear two signatures before the KOR can file and stamp them:

  • Bylaws Committee Chairman — certifies the committee’s compliance review and recommendation
  • Chapter Polemarch — confirms the chapter’s adoption authority and the legitimacy of the process

No other signatures are required or substitutable. Both must be present on the same document page(s) that contain the adopted language.

KOR as Sole Authority

After applying the file date stamp, the Keeper of Records is the sole authority for the official version of the DLA Bylaws. No other person or body may maintain or distribute a version that supersedes the KOR’s official record. Members needing the current bylaws should always access the version posted to the member portal by the KOR.

Portal Posting Timeline: The updated bylaws document must be posted to the member portal within 5 business days of the KOR’s file stamp. Chapter notification at the next regular meeting must also cite the amendment ID and effective date.
