About This Guide

This operations guide is the authoritative reference for the Denton-Lewisville (TX) Alumni Chapter Military & Veterans Affairs Commission (MVAC). Commission Commitment

"Honor the Uniform. Serve the Brotherhood." — Every program we execute reflects our obligation to the men who served this nation and to the fraternal values of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc.

Introduction

Introduction & Background

Purpose of the Commission, its place within the chapter, and the military heritage of Kappa Alpha Psi.

Purpose of This Guide

The MVAC Operations Guide is the authoritative reference for all matters related to the Military & Veterans Affairs Commission of the Denton-Lewisville (TX) Alumni Chapter of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc. It establishes the policies, procedures, programs, and standards that govern how the Commission honors, connects, and empowers military brothers across all five program areas.

This guide is not simply a program outline. It is an operational framework ensuring that every Commission member, chapter officer, program volunteer, and community partner engages with military brothers with precision, intentionality, and excellence — every time.

Commission Background

The Military & Veterans Affairs Commission was established to address a recognized gap in fraternal programming: while Kappa Alpha Psi has always counted military men among its brotherhood, structured programming to identify, engage, and serve those brothers had not been formalized at the chapter level. The DLA MVAC closes that gap.

The Commission draws its mandate from Kappa Alpha Psi's long military legacy — seven of the Fraternity's founders served in the United States military — and from the chapter's commitment to "Achievement in Every Field of Human Endeavor," including the field of military service.

Who Should Read This Guide

  • MVAC Chair and Commission leadership
  • Chapter officers and board members
  • Program volunteers and event coordinators
  • Community partners and corporate sponsors
  • Incoming officers and future commission leadership
Guiding Principle

Every veteran brother who walks through the chapter's door should leave feeling seen, valued, and connected. The MVAC exists to make that standard a reality — not an aspiration.

Chapter 1

Legacy & Founders

The military heritage of Kappa Alpha Psi and the seven founders who served in uniform.

1.1 Fraternity Military Heritage

Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc., was founded on January 5, 1911, at Indiana University in Bloomington, Indiana — a time when African American men faced profound barriers to education, service, and recognition. Despite those barriers, the founders of Kappa Alpha Psi chose to serve: their nation, their communities, and each other.

Of the Fraternity's ten founders, seven served in the United States military. Their service during World War I and beyond established a legacy of patriotism, sacrifice, and Brotherhood that continues to define the Fraternity's relationship with military service to this day.

1.2 The Seven Military Founders

John Milton Lee
U.S. Army — WWI
Polemarch of Alpha Chapter (1911–1912). Among the first founders to enter military service. His leadership shaped the Fraternity's early identity and its commitment to honorable achievement.
Elder Watson Diggs
U.S. Army — WWI
Principal founder and first Grand Polemarch. Visionary architect of the Fraternity who served his country while building an institution meant to endure.
Byron Kenneth Armstrong
U.S. Army — WWI
Scholar and founder who exemplified the Fraternity's commitment to academic achievement alongside military duty.
Ezra Dee Alexander
U.S. Army — WWI
One of the charter members who helped establish the Brotherhood's foundational values of service and scholarship.
Henry Tourner Asher
U.S. Army — WWI
Founder who embodied the intersection of intellectual achievement and national service that defines Kappa Alpha Psi.
Marcus Peter Blakemore
U.S. Army — WWI
Founder and early chapter leader whose military service reinforced the Fraternity's ethos of giving back to the nation.
Paul Waymond Caine
U.S. Army — WWI
Founder whose service set the template for military brothers who would follow in the decades to come.
Commission Commitment

The MVAC honors the legacy of these seven men by ensuring that every military brother in the Denton-Lewisville chapter is identified, engaged, and celebrated. Their sacrifice obligates us to serve.

1.3 DLA Chapter Military Heritage

The Denton-Lewisville (TX) Alumni Chapter, chartered in 1997, serves a region with a significant military population — including veterans, active-duty personnel, and military families connected to the Dallas-Fort Worth metroplex military installations and the broader veteran community of Denton and Tarrant counties.

The chapter has consistently counted military brothers among its leadership and membership. The establishment of the MVAC formalizes what has always been true: military service is a cornerstone of this Brotherhood.

Chapter 5 — Area I

Brother Engagement

Identifying, connecting, and integrating military brothers into active chapter life.

5.1 Military Brother Census

The foundation of all MVAC programming is an accurate, current census of military brothers in the chapter. Without knowing who our veteran and active-duty brothers are, we cannot serve them. The census is not a one-time effort — it is an ongoing responsibility.

Policy

All chapter members who have served or are serving in the military are strongly encouraged to complete the MVAC Military Census Registration. The MVAC Chair maintains the census database and updates it at each chapter meeting.

Census Data Collected

  • Branch of service (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Coast Guard, Space Force, Guard/Reserve)
  • Era of service (Vietnam, Gulf War, OIF/OEF, post-9/11, etc.)
  • MOS/Rate/Specialty (optional)
  • Rank at separation or current rank
  • Combat deployment history (optional)
  • Transition assistance needs
  • Interest in MVAC leadership or volunteer roles

5.2 Outreach Strategy

Step 1
Roster Review
MVAC Chair reviews chapter roster annually to identify brothers with known military backgrounds.
Step 2
Personal Contact
Each identified brother receives a personal call or visit from the MVAC Chair — not a mass email.
Step 3
Census Completion
Brothers are invited to complete the census form and asked about specific interests and needs.
Step 4
Program Matching
Based on census data, brothers are matched with relevant programs, mentors, and recognition opportunities.
Step 5
Annual Re-engagement
Census is refreshed annually; previously inactive brothers are re-contacted with updated program opportunities.

5.3 Recognition Within the Chapter

  • Military brothers are recognized at the annual Veterans Day chapter meeting
  • Service branch affiliations are noted in chapter communications with permission
  • Military milestones (promotions, retirements, deployments, returns) are announced at chapter meetings
  • Military brothers are given ceremonial recognition during chapter programs when appropriate
Chapter 6 — Area II

Community Service

Public-facing programs serving the broader North Texas veteran community.

6.1 Veterans Day Program

The chapter's annual Veterans Day program is the MVAC's signature public event. It is held in November and serves as the primary public expression of the chapter's commitment to honoring military service.

Program Standard

The Veterans Day program must include: (1) formal recognition of all military brothers present by branch, (2) a keynote or featured veteran speaker, (3) recognition of Gold Star families if present, and (4) public acknowledgment of the Mortally Wounded Project honorees.

6.2 Stand Down Event

The annual Stand Down event — held in October — provides direct services to at-risk and homeless veterans in the North Texas community. Services may include benefits assistance, hygiene kits, clothing distribution, employment resources, and mental health referrals.

6.3 Military Spouse Appreciation

Each April, the MVAC coordinates recognition of military spouses — acknowledging the sacrifice, resilience, and service of the families behind the uniform. This may include a chapter social event, public recognition, or partnership with military family service organizations.

6.4 Memorial Day Commemoration

The MVAC coordinates chapter participation in local Memorial Day observances, cemetery visits, or wreath-laying ceremonies to honor the fallen — with particular emphasis on recognizing brothers memorialized in the Mortally Wounded Project.

6.5 Patriot Day Recognition

On September 11, the Commission coordinates a moment of chapter acknowledgment honoring the lives lost and the service members who answered the call in the years that followed.

Chapter 7 — Area III

Military Mentorship

Transition assistance, peer mentoring, and veteran-to-veteran support networks.

7.1 Military-to-Civilian Transition Support

The transition from military service to civilian life is one of the most challenging periods a veteran faces. The MVAC maintains a network of brothers who have successfully navigated this transition and are available to mentor those who are navigating it now.

Transition Support Areas

  • Career translation and resume development
  • VA benefits navigation (healthcare, education, housing)
  • Entrepreneurship and small business development
  • Graduate education and certification pathways
  • Mental health resources and peer support connections
  • Networking and professional development

7.2 Peer Mentorship Program

The MVAC pairs newly identified veteran brothers with an established chapter member who shares a similar branch of service or civilian career field. These peer mentorship relationships are structured but informal — grounded in Brotherhood rather than bureaucracy.

Mentorship Principle

A veteran brother who is struggling — financially, professionally, or emotionally — should never have to struggle alone while he has brothers in his chapter. The mentorship program exists to close that gap before it becomes a crisis.

Chapter 8 — Area IV

Guide Right & Youth

Military STEM academies, JROTC partnerships, and youth leadership programs.

8.1 Military STEM Academy

The MVAC partners with Guide Right to deliver Military STEM programming to youth in the Denton-Lewisville area. These programs use military-inspired project-based learning to teach STEM concepts while building the discipline, teamwork, and leadership skills that define military culture.

Program Elements

  • Engineering and robotics challenges modeled on military problem-solving frameworks
  • Cybersecurity and technology workshops aligned with military occupational specialties
  • Leadership development seminars delivered by veteran brothers
  • College and career pathway counseling with emphasis on military education benefits

8.2 JROTC Partnership

The Commission maintains active partnerships with JROTC programs at Denton-area high schools. See Chapter 12 for full JROTC Partnership details.

8.3 Youth Leadership Pipeline

Military-connected youth who complete MVAC programming are considered priority candidates for Guide Right mentorship, Kappa League participation, and — as they reach adulthood — fraternity membership. The Commission maintains relationships with these young men across their developmental years.

Pipeline Standard

Every young man who participates in an MVAC youth program should leave with: (1) a clear picture of what military service offers, (2) at least one veteran brother as a personal contact, and (3) knowledge of the chapter's Guide Right programs.

Chapter 9 — Area V

Strategic Partnerships

Corporate, government, and nonprofit alliances amplifying veteran impact.

9.1 Partnership Philosophy

The MVAC cannot accomplish its mission alone. Strategic partnerships multiply the Commission's reach, resources, and credibility — connecting our military brothers to opportunities and organizations that a single chapter could not access independently.

9.2 Corporate Partnerships

The Commission seeks relationships with employers who are committed to veteran hiring and advancement. Corporate partners may support the Commission through:

  • Employment and internship opportunities for transitioning veterans
  • Financial sponsorship of MVAC events
  • In-kind donations of goods and services for Stand Down and community events
  • Professional development workshops and hiring fairs

9.3 Government Agency Partners

  • Texas Veterans Commission
  • Dallas VA Medical Center and Community-Based Outpatient Clinics
  • U.S. Department of Labor Veterans' Employment and Training Service (VETS)
  • Local Veterans Service Organizations (VSOs)
  • Denton County Veterans Services Office

9.4 Nonprofit Partners

  • Disabled American Veterans (DAV)
  • Veterans of Foreign Wars (VFW) — local posts
  • American Legion — local posts
  • Warrior-Gateway and other veteran navigation nonprofits
  • Military family support organizations

9.5 Scholarship Initiatives

The Commission works with chapter leadership and corporate partners to identify and promote scholarship opportunities specifically available to veteran brothers and military-connected youth — including GI Bill supplements, employer education benefits, and veteran-specific scholarship programs.

Chapter 10

Signature Programs

The Commission's flagship annual programs and key initiatives.

10.1 Signature Program Standards

A Signature Program is a recurring, annually scheduled program that represents the Commission's highest level of investment and visibility. Each Signature Program has a designated MVAC lead, an established event format, and measurable outcomes tracked year over year.

Program 1
Veterans Day Recognition Gala

Annual November event recognizing all military brothers by name, branch, and era of service. Includes keynote address, awards presentation, and public recognition ceremony. The chapter's premier military affairs event of the year.

Program 2
Veterans Stand Down

Annual October event providing direct services to at-risk and homeless veterans in North Texas. Coordinated with VA, VSOs, and community nonprofits. Targets brothers in need and extends service to the broader veteran community.

Program 3
Military STEM Academy

Summer youth program combining STEM education with military values and mentorship. Open to youth ages 12–18 in the Denton-Lewisville area. Delivered in partnership with Guide Right and veteran brothers.

Program 4
Military Spouse Appreciation Day

Annual April event honoring the families behind the uniform. Recognition, fellowship, and resource-sharing for military spouses of chapter brothers and the broader community.

Program 5
MLK Service Day — Veteran Volunteerism

Annual January community service event aligned with Martin Luther King Jr. Day, with a specific focus on veteran volunteerism and community impact in North Texas.

Chapter 12

JROTC Partnerships

School-based JROTC mentorship, awards, and pipeline development.

12.1 Partnership Philosophy

JROTC programs develop the same virtues Kappa Alpha Psi was built on: discipline, leadership, service, and achievement. The Commission's JROTC partnerships are a natural extension of Guide Right — using military structure as a vehicle for youth development.

12.2 JROTC Partnership Activities

  • Annual JROTC awards presented by the MVAC Chair at unit awards ceremonies
  • Cadet mentorship visits by veteran brothers to partner schools
  • Drill and ceremony demonstrations and coaching by veteran brothers
  • Leadership seminars and career pathway workshops for senior cadets
  • College and military academy application support
  • Invitation to chapter Guide Right and youth events

12.3 MVAC JROTC Award

Each partner school is eligible to have one cadet recognized annually with the MVAC Award for Excellence in Leadership and Service — presented at the school's awards ceremony by an MVAC representative and documented in chapter records.

Award Criteria

The MVAC JROTC Award is given to the cadet who best exemplifies leadership, service, academic achievement, and personal character — the same virtues embodied by the founders of Kappa Alpha Psi.

12.4 Target Partner Schools

The Commission prioritizes partnerships with JROTC programs at schools within the Denton and Lewisville Independent School Districts, with preference for programs that serve historically underrepresented students.

Chapter 14

Mortally Wounded Project

Preserving the eternal memory of brothers of Kappa Alpha Psi who gave the ultimate sacrifice in service to our nation.

Sacred Obligation

"A man is not truly gone as long as his name is spoken." — The Mortally Wounded Project ensures that brothers who died in service to this nation are never forgotten within the Brotherhood they gave everything to build.

14.1 Project Purpose

The Mortally Wounded Project is a permanent, living record of all known brothers of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc. — of any chapter — who died while serving in the United States Armed Forces. The DLA chapter maintains and expands its local list and contributes information to the national fraternity record.

14.2 Honor Roll Criteria

  • Brother must have been an initiated member of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc.
  • Brother must have died while on active duty or as a direct result of service-connected causes
  • Death must be verifiable through military records, news accounts, or family attestation
  • All branches of service and all eras of conflict are included

14.3 Annual Observance

The names of all Mortally Wounded Project honorees are read aloud at the chapter's annual Veterans Day program. This Memorial Roll Call is a solemn, central element of the program — not an afterthought.

14.4 How to Submit a Name

Brothers with knowledge of a member of Kappa Alpha Psi who died in military service should contact the MVAC Chair with the brother's name, chapter affiliation, branch of service, and any verifiable details. The MVAC Chair will research, document, and formally add the name to the honor roll.

Contact

Submit information to the MVAC Chair at the chapter meeting or via email. No submission is too old, too small, or too uncertain to pursue. If a brother served and died, he belongs on the roll.

Chapter 15

Service Standard

The Commission's standard of excellence for every program, event, and interaction.

15.1 The MVAC Service Standard

Every veteran brother who engages with the MVAC — at an event, through a mentorship connection, or simply at a chapter meeting — should feel the following:

Five Commitments

1. Seen: We know who you are and that you served.
2. Honored: We acknowledge the weight of your service without diminishing it.
3. Connected: We have linked you with brothers who share your experience.
4. Supported: We have resources and relationships available to you.
5. Remembered: Your name and service will outlast your years in this chapter.

15.2 Event Standards

  • All MVAC events begin and end on time
  • Military courtesies are observed appropriately (flag ceremony, national anthem, moment of silence)
  • Veterans are greeted by name whenever possible
  • Every event concludes with a clear call to action for continued engagement
  • Feedback is solicited from participants after every major event

15.3 Interaction Standards

  • Never ask a veteran "did you kill anyone?" or similar inappropriate questions
  • Never assume the nature or difficulty of a brother's service
  • Always thank a brother for their service — but then go deeper: ask what they did, what they're proud of, what they need
  • Respect that some service-connected experiences are private and should not be pressed
  • When in doubt, lead with Brotherhood — not with military rank or status
Chapter 16

Communications Protocol

Internal and external communications standards for the Commission.

16.1 Internal Communications

The MVAC Chair reports to the chapter Polemarch and provides a written Commission report at each chapter meeting. The report includes: census updates, upcoming events, partnership developments, and any recognition nominations.

16.2 External Communications

All external MVAC communications — press releases, social media posts, partner correspondence — must be coordinated with the chapter's Marketing & Communications Director (MAC) per the DLA Media Guide. The MVAC does not issue communications independently without MAC coordination.

Policy

The MVAC Chair is the Commission's sole authorized spokesperson. Commission members do not speak to media or external organizations on behalf of the Commission without the Chair's authorization.

16.3 Social Media Standards

  • Military brother recognition posts require the written consent of the brother being featured
  • Event photos require standard chapter photo release procedures
  • All MVAC social media content uses approved chapter hashtags: #DLAKappa #DLANupes #MVACserves
  • Do not post graphic content related to combat or injury
  • Mortally Wounded Project honorees may be featured in memorial posts on Veterans Day and Memorial Day

16.4 Veteran-Sensitive Communication

MVAC communications about brothers in distress — financial need, mental health, housing — are handled with strict confidentiality. The MVAC Chair connects struggling brothers with resources directly; their situations are never discussed publicly or in general chapter communications without explicit permission.

Chapter 17

Commission Structure

Leadership roles, committee organization, and governance framework.

MVAC Chair ▾
  • Oversees all Commission programs and activities
  • Maintains the military brother census database
  • Serves as liaison between the Commission, chapter officers, and external partners
  • Provides written report at each chapter meeting
  • Submits national award nominations annually

  • Manages event timelines, vendor relationships, and volunteer assignments
  • Maintains the annual program calendar and tracks milestone deadlines
Outreach & Mentorship Lead ▾
  • Manages the military brother census outreach campaign
  • Coordinates peer mentorship pairings
  • Serves as primary point of contact for transitioning veteran brothers
Youth & JROTC Liaison ▾
  • Manages relationships with JROTC partner schools
  • Coordinates Military STEM Academy programming with Guide Right
  • Administers the annual MVAC JROTC Award
Partnerships Coordinator ▾

  • Manages partner communications and stewardship

The MVAC operates under the authority of the chapter Polemarch and Board of Directors. The Chair is appointed by the Polemarch for a one-year term, renewable by mutual agreement. Commission members are volunteers from the chapter membership; military service is preferred but not required for all roles.

Chapter 20

Implementation Phases

Year-one through year-three rollout plan and milestones for Commission development.

20.1 Phased Implementation

Building the MVAC from a concept to a fully operational Commission requires a deliberate, phased approach. Each phase has clear deliverables and sets the foundation for the next.

Phase One — Foundation (Year 1)

1

Commission Formation

Appoint MVAC Chair; recruit initial commission members; establish governance framework.

2

Military Census Launch

Conduct chapter-wide census; build initial database of military brothers.

3

First Signature Events

Execute Veterans Day Recognition program and Memorial Roll Call.

4

Mortally Wounded Project Launch

Research and document initial honor roll; establish annual Roll Call tradition.

5

First JROTC Partnership

Establish relationship with at least one JROTC program; present first MVAC Award.

Phase Two — Growth (Year 2)

1

All Five Program Areas Active

Launch programming in all five areas; assign area leads.

2

Military STEM Academy Launch

First summer Military STEM Academy in partnership with Guide Right.

3

First Stand Down Event

Execute first Veterans Stand Down in partnership with VA and community nonprofits.

4

Corporate Partnership Program

Formalize first corporate partnership; launch veteran hiring initiative.

Phase Three — Maturity (Year 3)

1

Full Calendar Execution

All 12 monthly programs and observances executed on schedule.

2

National Recognition

Submit at least one program for national fraternity recognition.

3

Model Chapter Status

Share best practices and serve as a resource for other chapters developing MVAC programs.

4

Succession Planning

Identify and develop next generation of MVAC leadership from within the military brother community.

Chapter 21

Vision

The Commission's long-term vision for military brotherhood and community impact in North Texas.

21.1 The Commission's Vision

Vision Statement

To be recognized as the premier fraternal military affairs program in North Texas — a model of excellence that every Kappa Alpha Psi chapter and every veteran-serving organization in the region points to as the standard for honoring those who served.

21.2 Five-Year Goals

  • Every military brother in the chapter identified, engaged, and connected to at least one MVAC program
  • A fully documented Mortally Wounded Project honor roll with annual ceremony
  • Military STEM Academy serving 50+ youth annually
  • JROTC partnerships at three or more schools
  • An endowed scholarship for veteran brothers and military-connected youth
  • National fraternity recognition for at least one MVAC program
  • The Commission serving as a mentoring resource for MVAC programs at other chapters

21.3 The North Star

What We're Building Toward

A Denton-Lewisville where no veteran brother feels invisible — where no military family struggles alone — where every JROTC cadet in our community knows that fraternal men of distinction are invested in their future. That is what the MVAC is building. Not programs. Community.

21.4 The Five Commitments — Renewed

We will HONOR the uniform, the sacrifice, and the humanity of every brother who served.
We will SERVE veteran brothers with the same discipline and commitment they brought to their service.
We will CONNECT military brothers to each other and to the community that needs their leadership.
We will DEVELOP the next generation of military-minded leaders through Guide Right and our JROTC partnerships.
We will REMEMBER those who gave all — carrying their names forward as long as this chapter stands.
