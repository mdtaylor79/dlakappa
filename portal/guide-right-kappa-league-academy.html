Guide Right vs. Kappa League

Guide Right has been the Fraternity's service program since 1922. Kappa League sits inside it, and that structure explains the certification, the reporting, and the standards you are held to.

The relationship in one line

Guide Right is the Fraternity's national service program. Kappa League is the youth leadership component of Guide Right. The chapter delivers both.

A brief history

Conceived in 1922 by Leon W. Steward and suggested at the twelfth Grand Chapter of Kappa Alpha Psi, Guide Right became the Fraternity's national service program. Leon Steward is the founder of the National Guide Right movement of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc., and a Past Western Province Polemarch.

Soon after the St. Louis Alumni Chapter's beginning in 1921, Leon W. Steward and J. Jerome Peters were assigned to research possible programs that would assist in developing the youth of the Black community and to devise a "meaningful and practical approach" to the problem of Black youth underachievement. Brother Steward ultimately proposed a program of guidance to be designated as Guide Right, with the purpose of assisting high school seniors to choose and pursue useful careers. The focus of Guide Right, from its inception, was to provide scholarships to needy and talented students and to inform young people of the professions and other career options.

Purpose and focus

Guide Right's main focus is the educational and occupational guidance of youth, primarily inspirational and informative in character. The purpose is to place the training, experience, and friendly interest of successful men at the disposal of youth needing inspiration and counsel. The service program aims to assist youth in finding their direction and to arouse the interest of the community in addressing the problems facing those trying to realize their life's purpose.

The program encompasses many of the Fraternity's youth-oriented programs, including mentoring, college preparatory programs, and tutoring.

The Guide Right Service Program is focused on the future through its flagship initiative, the Kappa Leadership Development League. That work carries six aims.

The thread from 1922 to now

Scholarships for talented students in need, and telling young people what careers exist and how to reach them. Those were Steward's two original aims, and they are still what the College Prep Series, College Success, and the Guide Right Foundation scholarships in Lesson 21 are built to do.

How Kappa League fits

Guide Right is the national service program of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc., created to promote educational and occupational guidance for youth. Kappa League is the youth leadership development program operating under Guide Right, aimed at developing young men's leadership skills.

That structure matters in practice. A chapter can operate a Guide Right program without a Kappa League component, and the national certification form asks that question directly. The Denton-Lewisville Alumni Chapter operates both, which means the chapter answers the full certification form every year.

Where people get it wrong

Guide Right and Kappa League are not two names for the same thing, and Kappa League is not a separate organization the chapter also happens to run. Every Kappa League activity is a Guide Right activity and carries every Guide Right requirement: the background checks, the training, the insurance filing, and the certification. The reverse is not true. A chapter can serve youth through Guide Right without ever chartering a Kappa League.

Mentors are integral to the Kappa League program. They provide guidance, support, and encouragement to mentees. They serve as role models and help mentees develop leadership skills, achieve academic success, and grow personally.

Mentor expectations

Role model and guide

Demonstrate the values of punctuality, dependability, and reliability. Be friendly, and hold a professional boundary. You are a mentor, not necessarily a friend. That distinction protects the young man and protects you.

Communication

Maintain regular communication with mentees and their families in an honest and respectful manner. Be transparent and attentive about time commitments. If your availability changes, say so early.

Diversity and inclusion

Respect and appreciate the diversity of mentees, including their religion, culture, and economic background. Encourage and support them in doing well academically and personally.

Training and development

Participate in mandatory training sessions provided by the program. Engage in continuous learning to improve mentoring effectiveness. Attend Guide Right and Kappa Man(tor) trainings as required.

The boundary, stated plainly

Friendly is not the same as friends. No private social media connections with a Leaguer. No one-on-one contact out of sight of another adult. No transportation of a single Leaguer alone. No gifts, loans, or financial arrangements. No contact that a parent would be surprised to learn about.

Clearance 1: The Tier 3 background check

All mentors and volunteers complete a Tier 3 background check, a comprehensive screening that goes beyond a standard criminal check. It includes a criminal history check, a sex offender registry check, a child abuse and neglect registry check, and fingerprinting. Fingerprints stay on file so verification is thorough and accurate. IdentoGO is the fingerprinting provider referenced by the chapter.

Chapters are required to institute a three-level background check process at the federal, state, and local levels. Refer to Texas guidelines for approved agencies.

Automatic disqualifiers

Prior history of abuse of children, sexual or otherwise. Conviction of any crime involving children. History of violence or sexually exploitive behavior. Termination from a paid or volunteer position caused by misconduct involving a child. Failure to disclose a felony conviction, particularly anything related to violence, abuse, or children.

Background checks are performed annually for everyone with ongoing youth access. Newly initiated military members are exempt from the annual check under the Guide Right Certification requirements, and undergraduate brothers are exempt from the yearly check on the chapter background check form.

Clearance 2: Youth Protection Training

Every mentor and volunteer completes the Boy Scouts of America Youth Protection Training. It is valid for two years from the date of completion. Print the completion certificate and submit it to the chapter Keeper of Records, who maintains the file. Module 3 covers the substance of the training.

The course is available at scouting.org/training/youth-protection.

Module 3: Youth Protection and Safety Essentials

The core content of Youth Protection Training, restated for the Kappa League setting. This module is required reading even if your certificate is current.

Module objectives

  • Apply two-deep leadership and no-one-on-one contact in real situations.
  • Recognize the signs of abuse and neglect.
  • Execute the reporting sequence without hesitation.

Overview

Youth Protection Training equips mentors and volunteers with the knowledge and skills to protect youth participants. It is mandatory for all mentors and valid for two years. Five topics carry the weight.

1. Recognizing signs of abuse

Understand the different types of abuse, physical, emotional, and sexual, along with neglect. Learn to identify signs of abuse in youth. You are not asked to diagnose or investigate. You are asked to notice and to report.

2. Prevention strategies

Implement safe practices that prevent abuse, including maintaining appropriate boundaries and avoiding one-on-one situations in isolated areas. Use the two-deep leadership model: two adults are always present during activities.

3. Reporting procedures

Know the mandatory reporting requirements for suspected abuse, and understand how to report concerns to authorities and to program coordinators. Mandated reporting is required of all mentors and volunteers.

4. Communication guidelines

Maintain open and appropriate communication with youth and their families. Use respectful and inclusive language. Keep communication in channels that other adults can see.

5. Creating a safe environment

Establish a welcoming and inclusive atmosphere for all participants. Monitor and supervise activities so that participant safety is maintained throughout.

The reporting sequence

  1. Ensure immediate safety. If a young person is in danger right now, call 911.
  2. Report to the authorities. Mandated reporting is a personal legal duty. Do not delegate it, and do not wait for the chapter to decide. In Texas, suspected abuse or neglect is reported to the Texas Department of Family and Protective Services.
  3. Notify the Program Director and Safety and Security Officer. Same day, without exception.
  4. Document. Complete an incident report with facts, times, and quotes. Record what was said and observed, not conclusions.
  5. Protect confidentiality. Discuss only with those who need to know. Do not discuss it with other Leaguers, other parents, or on any channel.

Never promise secrecy

If a Leaguer says "promise you won't tell anyone," the honest answer is that you will keep what he shares private except when someone's safety is at risk, and that you will tell him what you have to do before you do it. Saying that up front protects the relationship when the moment comes.

Certification process

  • Complete the online Youth Protection Training module.
  • Print the training completion certificate.
  • Submit the certificate to the chapter Keeper of Records.
  • Repeat every two years, tracked against the certification deadline in Lesson 26.

Knowledge check

During a service project, a Leaguer tells you his stepfather hits him when he drinks, then says "don't say anything, it's fine." What is your first obligation?

Correct answer: A. Mandated reporting is immediate and personal. You do not investigate, you do not contact the alleged abuser, and you do not wait. Tell the young man honestly what you are doing.

Module 4: Building and Cultivating the Relationship

Trust first, goals second, feedback throughout. The mechanics of a mentoring relationship that lasts past the program year.

Module objectives

  • Open a mentoring relationship in a way that builds trust quickly.
  • Write a SMART goal with a mentee and review it on a schedule.
  • Give feedback that a teenager can act on.

Establishing trust

Step 1

Initial engagement

Begin with a welcoming and open introduction that sets a positive tone. Listen actively to understand his needs, interests, and background before offering advice.

Step 2

Consistency and reliability

Be consistent in your interactions and maintain regular contact. Follow through on every commitment. A mentor who shows up is already ahead of most adults in a young man's week.

Step 3

Respect and empathy

Show respect for his opinions and experiences. Practice empathy by working to understand his perspective and the pressures he is carrying.

Identifying goals and objectives

Work collaboratively with your mentee to identify short-term and long-term goals. Goals should be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. Focus on academic and personal development together, and use goal setting to motivate rather than to grade.

Conduct regular check-ins to assess progress. Be flexible and adjust goals when circumstances change. The goal-setting worksheet in Lesson 31 gives you the structure to run this conversation.

Turning a vague intention into a SMART goal.
He saysYou write together
"I want to do better in school."Raise my Algebra II grade from a 74 to an 85 by the end of the second nine weeks, by attending tutoring every Tuesday and turning in every assignment on time.
"I might go to college."Build a list of six colleges with application deadlines and test requirements by December 15, and register for the March SAT by February 1.
"I need to stop being shy."Deliver the 30-second elevator pitch at the November session and volunteer to lead one small group discussion before winter break.

Regular interaction and feedback

Scheduled meetings

Establish a regular meeting schedule so engagement is consistent rather than occasional. Use meetings to discuss progress, challenges, and upcoming opportunities.

Constructive feedback

Provide feedback that is specific and actionable. Encourage self-reflection and critical thinking by asking thought-provoking questions rather than delivering verdicts. "What would you do differently next time" beats "that was wrong."

Celebrating success

Recognize and celebrate achievements, however small, to build confidence and motivation. Use positive reinforcement to encourage continued effort. Tell the parent about the win too.

Building a lasting relationship

  • Open communication. Create an environment where he feels comfortable sharing thoughts and concerns, and encourage him to take an active role in his own development.
  • Life reflection and gratitude. Teach the value of gratitude and reflection as tools for growth. Ask him to identify what he is grateful for and to reflect on the experiences that shaped him.
  • Long-term impact. Aim for a relationship that extends past the program year. Encourage him to become a mentor himself and pass on what he gained.

Questions that open a session

What went well this week that nobody noticed? What is the hardest class right now and why? Who do you go to when something goes wrong? What are you looking forward to? What do you want to be good at by this time next year?

Knowledge check

Your mentee has missed his goal three months running. What is the strongest next move?

Correct answer: B. Regular review with flexibility to adjust is the stated practice. Lowering the bar without a conversation teaches him nothing, and outsourcing it to his parents ends the mentoring relationship.

Module 5: Communication, Culture, and Conflict

Listening well, respecting difference, and handling the moments when a session goes sideways.

Module objectives

  • Use active listening techniques in a mentoring conversation.
  • Recognize and respect the diverse backgrounds of Leaguers and families.
  • Resolve conflict between Leaguers without escalating it.

Communication that works with teenagers

Listen more than you talk

Ask, wait, and let silence do work. A three-second pause after a question produces a better answer than a follow-up question.

Reflect what you heard

"So the issue is not the class, it is that you cannot get to tutoring because of practice." Naming it accurately proves you were listening.

Be concrete

Replace "apply yourself" with "email Ms. Rivera tonight and ask for the makeup work." Advice a young man can act on this week is worth more than advice about his character.

Keep families informed

Communicate with mentees and their families in an honest and respectful manner. Parents who hear from you regularly become your strongest support.

Cultural competency and diversity

Recognize and respect the diverse backgrounds and experiences of mentees, including religion, culture, and economic circumstances. Foster an inclusive environment that values that diversity. Practically, that means asking rather than assuming: about family structure, about what a young man can afford to participate in, about religious observance that affects a Saturday activity, and about the accommodation a family needs for attire at a formal event.

Cost is a barrier no one announces

Program-specific attire, event fees, and transportation are real obstacles. Build activities so participation never depends on what a family can spend, and route any need quietly through the Program Director.

Conflict resolution and problem solving

  1. Separate and settle. Stop the exchange, separate the parties, and give everyone a minute. Nothing gets solved hot.
  2. Hear each side alone. Two-deep leadership applies here as much as anywhere. Get the facts from each young man without an audience.
  3. Name the actual issue. Most conflicts between Leaguers are about respect in front of peers, not about the incident.
  4. Let them build the fix. Ask each what a fair resolution looks like. Encourage critical thinking and problem-solving rather than issuing a ruling.
  5. Close the loop. Confirm the agreement, tell the Program Director, and document anything that involved threats, physical contact, or harassment.

Not negotiable

Hazing, bullying, and harassment in any form are strictly prohibited, as is discriminatory language or action based on race, gender, religion, or other protected characteristics. These go to the Program Director immediately and follow the disciplinary process in Lesson 27, not the conflict resolution steps above.

Knowledge check

Two Leaguers trade insults during a group discussion and one uses a slur. What happens next?

Correct answer: C. Discriminatory language is a code of conduct violation, not an interpersonal disagreement. It is addressed openly so every Leaguer in the room sees the standard, then escalated and documented.

Community service hours, done right

Community service is integral to the program. It builds civic responsibility, and accurate tracking makes sure participants receive recognition for their contributions while the chapter can assess its community impact.

  1. Eligibility. Activities must be organized or approved by the Kappa League program or its partners, and must contribute to the well-being of the community in line with program goals.
  2. Documentation. Mentees record their own hours in a logbook or digital tracker. Every entry carries date, activity description, location, hours worked, and a supervisor's signature.
  3. Submission. Logs go to the Community Service Coordinator monthly or quarterly. Entries must be complete and signed before submission.
  4. Verification. The Coordinator reviews and verifies each log. Verified hours enter the mentee's program record and count toward recognition and awards.

Why this matters beyond the paperwork

Verified hours support the Community Service Award, feed the chapter's 100-hour target, and appear in the annual Guide Right Certification. Hours that were served but never logged do not exist for any of those purposes.

Confidentiality of records

  • All personal information about mentees and their families is confidential.
  • Use discretion when discussing participants, and never share sensitive information with people who have no role in the program.
  • Program coordinators store records containing confidential information securely.
  • Confidentiality holds except as required by law or program policy, which includes mandated reporting.

Kappa League Do's and Don'ts

The national guardrails that keep the program focused on mentoring and development. Signed acknowledgment is required before Guide Right certification.

These guidelines exist for mentors, volunteers, and chapter members so the program keeps its focus on mentoring and development. They outline acceptable and unacceptable practice, and they carry compliance weight.

Do's

  1. Focus on mentorship. Prioritize the mentorship and development of Kappa Leaguers through structured programs and activities. Encourage positive behavior and personal growth in every interaction.
  2. Adhere to the mission. Align all activities with the primary purpose of the Kappa League, which is to mentor and develop young men through the Four Major Initiatives and the Seven Phases.
  3. Promote leadership and education. Facilitate programs that build leadership skills, academic success, and community involvement, and support Leaguers in setting and achieving goals.
  4. Maintain professionalism. Conduct yourself professionally when representing the Kappa League, and respect the confidentiality and privacy of all participants.
  5. Ensure safety and compliance. Follow safety protocols during activities, and make sure all mentors and volunteers are compliant with background check and training requirements.

Don'ts

The Denton-Lewisville Alumni Chapter Guide Right Program acknowledges that its Kappa League Program will not:

  • Utilize line names or line numbers
  • Utilize crossing jackets or any clothes with line names or line numbers
  • Utilize hand signs to identify or signify the Kappa League Program
  • Conduct or have probates or coming out shows
  • Utilize masks or costumes that resemble items of apparel used in a college probate show
  • Produce or distribute adult-like or inappropriate flyers for fundraisers or events
  • Conduct any pledging, initiation, or hazing activities
  1. Do not treat Kappa Leaguers as fraternity members. They are not junior Kappas and are not subjected to fraternity-like activities or rituals.
  2. Avoid hazing or inappropriate behavior. Do not engage in or tolerate any form of hazing, bullying, or inappropriate behavior. These are strictly prohibited and contrary to the values of the Kappa League.
  3. Do not deviate from the program's purpose. Refrain from activities that do not align with mentoring and leadership development. Every event and program carries a clear educational or developmental focus.
  4. Avoid unprofessional conduct. Do not engage in behavior that could be perceived as unprofessional or disrespectful to Kappa Leaguers, parents, guardians, guests, or other participants. Maintain a positive and supportive environment at all times.

Note on performances

The National Guide Right Commission does not encourage step shows, shimmying, or cane performances for Guide Right students. If a chapter elects to participate in these activities, the performances must be in good taste with no profanity, no inappropriate actions, and no sexually explicit movements, and must not utilize masks or anything that resembles a college probate show.

Acknowledgment and compliance

  • Each Kappa League program, Guide Right Chairman, Kappa League Chairman, Polemarch, and Keeper of Records must acknowledge the Do's and Don'ts before being Guide Right certified.
  • Chapter members, especially those volunteering to mentor Kappa Leaguers, must be aware of and adhere to these guidelines.
  • This acknowledgment is available on the National Guide Right website and with the Southwestern Province Guide Right Certification.

Kappa League Constitution and Bylaws

The governing documents of the Kappa Instructional Leadership League, reproduced in full.

Constitution

Purpose. The Constitution provides the foundational framework for the governance and operation of the Kappa League, outlining the organization's name, purpose, membership, and operational guidelines. It ensures that all participants understand the rules and structure governing the program.

Article I · Name

Section 1. The name of this organization shall be the Kappa Instructional Leadership League, to be called the Kappa League.

Article II · Purpose

Section 1. The objectives of this organization shall be to promote and develop the leadership potential of its members through participation in club-oriented activities and to motivate them to raise their aspiration levels.

Article III · Membership

Section 1. The organization's membership shall consist of male students who are properly enrolled and meet the specifications of the membership of the Kappa League.

Section 2. All members shall be privileged to participate in all Kappa League activities and elections, except when said privilege is suspended by reason of the members' failure to maintain the minimum requirements.

Article IV · Officers

Section 1. The organization's officers shall be a president, first vice president, second vice president, third vice president, secretary, treasurer, and five directors of Area Phases.

Section 2. A presidential vacancy shall be filled in order of succession beginning with the first vice-president, the second vice-president, and finally, if necessary, a special Kappa League election.

Section 3. These officers shall hold office for no more than one year.

Section 4. All officers will be elected by the entire membership during elections held in May of each year.

Section 5. The qualifications and duties for each office are to be determined by the Kappa League members' Bylaws as approved by the Kappa League members.

Section 6. No officer may hold more than one seat in the Kappa League for the duration of one year.

Article V · Executive and legislative powers

Section 1. The legislative powers shall be vested in the Board of Directors.

Section 2. The Board of Directors, consisting of the officers listed in Article IV, shall meet regularly and by majority vote shall pass Kappa League programs and projects to be carried out by the Kappa League. The Board of Directors, by majority vote, shall recommend proposed laws and regulations for that purpose.

Article VI · Amendments

Section 1. Either the Kappa League membership or the Board of Directors may draft proposed amendments to this Constitution.

Section 2. Upon arrival of the majority of members of the Kappa League Board of Directors, the proposed amendment must be approved by a two-thirds vote cast by the Kappa League membership in a special Kappa League meeting called specifically for that purpose.

Article VII · Bylaws

Section 1. The membership, by majority vote, shall determine the Bylaws of the Kappa League, which must not conflict with the content of the Constitution of the Kappa League.

Article VIII · Ratification

Section 1. The Constitution shall take effect when a two-thirds vote cast by the Kappa League membership ratifies it.

Article IX · Sponsorship

Section 1. The Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc., sponsors the Kappa League.

Section 2. The Kappa League shall engage in no programs or projects unless approved by the Fraternity appointed advisors.

Section 3. The Kappa League, without the written approval of the Fraternity appointed advisors, shall make no expenditure.

Bylaws of the Kappa League

Purpose. The Bylaws provide detailed operational guidelines and procedures supporting the implementation of the Constitution. They specify the duties and responsibilities of officers, outline meeting protocols, and set forth participation requirements, establishing clear expectations and standards for all members.

Article I · Duties of officers

Section 1. The President shall:

  1. Act as the chief executive officer of the Kappa League.
  2. Preside as a nonvoting member at all meetings of the Board of Directors and General Membership.
  3. Be an ex-officio member of all committees of the Kappa League.
  4. Be empowered to appoint any special committees for the general welfare of the Kappa League.

Section 2. The First Vice-President shall:

  1. Be in charge of programs and projects.
  2. Be an ex-officio member of all committees of (a) immediately above.
  3. Be an assistant to the President for all executive tasks.

Section 3. The Second Vice-President shall:

  1. Be in charge of the following areas of responsibility: Parliamentarian, Sergeant-at-Arms, Constitution, Bylaws, Historian, and Public Relations.
  2. Be an ex-officio member of all committees of (a) immediately above.
  3. Be an assistant to the President for all executive tasks.

Section 4. The Third Vice-President shall:

  1. Be in charge of membership recruitment and activation.
  2. Be an ex-officio member of all committees of (a) immediately above.
  3. Be an assistant to the President for all executive tasks.

Section 5. The Secretary shall:

  1. Keep minutes of all meetings of the Board of Directors and General Membership.
  2. Maintain a book of records containing the minutes listed in (a) immediately above.
  3. Handle all correspondence for the Kappa League.
  4. Be assisted by a corresponding and recording secretary whose duties shall be assigned by the Secretary, who has the power to appoint members to this position.

Section 6. The Treasurer shall:

  1. Be chairman of the Ways and Means Committee.
  2. Collect and record all dues and assessments.
  3. Be an ex-officio member of all fund-raising committees.
  4. Be assisted by a collection and recording Treasurer whose duties and appointments shall be made by the Treasurer.

Section 7. The Historian shall:

  1. Record in the Kappa League scrapbook all activities of historical interest.
  2. Maintain the Kappa League archives.

Section 8. The Director of Public Relations shall:

  1. Direct the Community Contact Committee of the Kappa League.
  2. Arrange the publicity for all Kappa League events.
  3. Direct the artwork and photography as they relate to publicity.
  4. Be in charge of the Kappa League bulletin board and showcase.

Section 9. The Parliamentarian shall:

  1. Make all decisions in parliamentary procedure.
  2. Direct all Kappa League elections.
Article II · Qualifications for office

Section 1. The Kappa League President must be a senior class member when in office and must have served at least one previous year on the Board of Directors.

Section 2. The First Vice-President must be in the senior class when serving in office.

Section 3. All other officers must be in at least the 10th grade when serving in office and have been a Kappa League member for at least six months.

Section 4. Members to be eligible for any Kappa League office must have at least a "C" average with no failures, acceptable deportment, cooperation, and work habits as recorded on the last official report card.

Article III · Meetings

Section 1. The Board of Directors will meet before each General Membership meeting. All committee chairmen proposing to make a report at the General Membership meeting must submit their report to the Board. Any Kappa League member is entitled to attend the Board meeting.

Section 2. The General Membership meetings will be held twice a month. One third of the membership constitutes a quorum to hold a meeting.

Section 3. All meetings will be held at The University of North Texas or authorized designated space.

Article IV · Participation requirements

Section 1. Minimum requirements for the privilege to participate in Kappa League activities and programs:

  1. Officers are required to attend each called meeting except when excused by the Kappa Advisor in advance of the time of said called meeting.
  2. New members must attend three General Membership meetings in succession to be eligible for a membership card.
  3. Any member absent from General Membership meetings for two successive meetings shall lose his voting rights.
  4. Any member absent from General Membership meetings for three successive meetings shall lose his privilege to attend all Kappa League activities. Such a member shall be reinstated only after having made an application to the Board of Directors and said application approved by the Board.
  5. Officers shall be excused from not more than two Board of Directors meetings and one General Membership meeting during his term of office.

Section 2. Dues.

  1. Dues shall be $150 per General Membership meeting. Members becoming delinquent in dues shall become subject to Article IV, Section 1, d, above.

Chapter note

Articles III and IV of the Bylaws are the provisions referenced in the attendance policy in Lesson 28. Direct Leaguers to those articles when a scheduling conflict comes up, and review the dues provision with the chapter each year before adopting the local Bylaws.

NGRC Certification Instructions

Step-by-step instructions for the National Guide Right Commission certification website, plus the chapter forms that carry signatures.

Greetings Chapter Guide Right Chairmen. The NGRC Certification Website is at https://kapsi-ngrc.org. Work through the sections in order, from registration to signatures.

Section 1: Registration

All Guide Right Committee members register themselves. Brothers who registered in a prior year still have accounts in the system and can use the "Forgot your Password" option on the login page. For brothers who have never registered:

  1. At the login page, type REGISTER-GR in all caps in the email field and click the Sign in button.
  2. Select your province from the drop-down list and click SELECT.
  3. Select your chapter from the drop-down list and click SELECT.
  4. Enter all the requested information, then click Submit Registration.
  5. Open the confirmation email and click the confirmation link. The account is now active and ready for login.

Section 2: Assigning roles

Before proceeding with certification, assign the appropriate brothers to the roles of Polemarch, Keeper of Records, Vice Chairman, and Kappa League Coordinator. The same brother may hold more than one role. Only the Guide Right Chairman can perform this task.

New Guide Right Chairman

If you are a new Guide Right Chairman or replacing the current one, contact your Province Guide Right Director so he can assign you as the Guide Right Chairman for your chapter.

  1. From the certification home page, click ASSIGN ROLES under the CERTIFICATION PROGRESS section.
  2. On the ROLE ASSIGNMENT page, use the four drop-down lists under Polemarch, Keeper of Records, Vice Chairman, and Kappa League Coordinator to select a brother for each role. If a brother is not in the list, send him the registration steps above.
  3. Once selections are made, click the ASSIGN ROLES button.
  4. Role assignment is complete. Return to the home screen.
  5. If the ASSIGN ROLES percentage on the home page is not 100 percent, return to the ROLE ASSIGNMENT page and complete all assignments.

If your chapter does not have a Guide Right Program, skip to Section 3.

Section 3: Entering certification details

This is the data entry portion of certification. Only the Guide Right Chairman or Vice Chairman can perform this task.

  1. From the certification home page, click ENTER CERTIFICATION DETAILS under the CERTIFICATION PROGRESS section.
  2. Answer all questions that apply to your program.
    • Guide Right program without a Kappa League component: answer YES to "Do you have a Guide Right Program," complete the top portion, answer NO to "Do you have a Kappa League Program," then Submit.
    • Guide Right program with a Kappa League component: answer YES to both questions, complete the top portion and all applicable questions in the bottom portion, then Submit.
    • No Guide Right program: answer NO to "Do you have a Guide Right Program," then Submit.
  3. If you do not have all the information, you can return to this page later.
  4. The home page displays your percent complete along with a list of answers that are missing.
  5. When the home page displays 100 percent, data entry is complete except for credentials. You can return to certification details to make changes.

Section 4: Acquiring signatures

Electronic signatures are required from the Guide Right Chairman, Polemarch, and Keeper of Records. All three receive emails when certification details are complete and ready to be signed. Only those three roles can perform this task.

  1. From the certification home page, click ACQUIRE SIGNATURES under the CERTIFICATION PROGRESS section.
  2. All information associated with your certification details is displayed. Read all responses, including the information about the operation of your Guide Right Program.
  3. At the bottom of the page, select "X" from the drop-down box next to your role. If you serve in more than one of the eligible roles, a drop-down box appears beside each role you hold.
  4. Click Submit.
  5. Certification is complete once all three roles have signed off.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from mentors, mentees, and parents, answered in one place.

About the program

What is the difference between Guide Right and Kappa League?

Guide Right is the national service program of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc., created to promote educational and occupational guidance for youth. Kappa League is a youth leadership development program that operates under Guide Right.

A chapter can run Guide Right without a Kappa League. A Kappa League cannot exist outside Guide Right. Every Kappa League activity is therefore a Guide Right activity and carries every Guide Right requirement, including background checks, Youth Protection Training, insurance filing, and annual certification. Lesson 2 has the full side-by-side comparison.

Who runs the program, the adults or the Leaguers?

Both, at different levels. The adult coordinating team operates the program and carries the authority and liability. The Leaguers elect their own Board of Directors each May and run their own meetings, programs, and projects under Fraternity-appointed advisors. Under Article IX of the Constitution, the League engages in no program and makes no expenditure without advisor approval. Lesson 5 covers the split.

For mentors

How do I become a mentor in the Kappa League program?

Complete the application process, including a Tier 3 background check and Youth Protection Training. Contact the Mentor Coordinator for more information. Module 2 covers the full sequence.

What is the time commitment for mentors?

Mentors commit to at least one academic year, with regular meetings and interactions with their mentees, plus monthly planning meetings.

How are mentors matched with mentees?

Matching is based on shared interests, goals, and compatibility, and is handled by the Mentor Coordinator.

Can I mentor if I am not a member of the Fraternity?

The program is supported by community volunteers, including Kappa League alumni, alongside chapter members. All adults meet the same screening and training standards regardless of membership.

For mentees

What activities can I expect to participate in?

Leadership workshops, community service projects, college and career exploration, and a range of skill-building activities across the seven phases.

How can I track my community service hours?

Use the Community Service Log in Lesson 31 to record your hours, and submit it to the Community Service Coordinator for verification.

Who do I contact with questions or concerns?

Reach out to your mentor or the appropriate Program Coordinator.

What happens if I miss meetings?

Under Article IV of the Bylaws, two successive absences from General Membership meetings cost you your voting rights, and three cost you the privilege of attending Kappa League activities until the Board approves your reinstatement application. Notify your mentor in advance when you cannot attend.

For parents and guardians

How can I support my child's participation?

Encourage active engagement in program activities and regular communication with his mentor. Attend program events and provide feedback to coordinators.

What measures are in place to ensure safety?

The program follows strict safety protocols including background checks for all volunteers, Youth Protection Training, two-deep leadership, and supervised activities.

How is information communicated to parents?

Through email, newsletters, social media updates, and the BAND app. Keep your contact information current with the program.

Is there a cost to participate?

Contact the Program Director about current dues, event costs, and available support. The program builds activities so that participation does not depend on what a family can spend.
