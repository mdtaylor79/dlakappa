Introduction & Purpose

Understand the founding authority of the Code, who is bound by it, and why it matters to every member of Kappa.

⏱ 10 min📚 Foundational
Learning Objectives

Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Incorporated® is a Greek-lettered organization with both collegiate and alumni chapters. Founded on January 5, 1911, on the campus of Indiana University in Bloomington, Indiana, the Fraternity has grown into a worldwide organization whose guiding achievement is Achievement.

Kappa expects every member to uphold and promote elevated cultural, intellectual, and moral standards — demonstrating through daily conduct the professional and personal values that embody the ideals and principles of the Fraternity.

Code Authority & Scope

Kappa adopted its Code of Conduct on June 30, 2011. This Code supersedes and replaces all prior disciplinary procedures, rules, and regulations previously in force. The Code exists not to punish brothers, but to protect the integrity of the Fraternity and the dignity of every member, applicant, and community member it interacts with.

Amendment Authority

Any amendments to this Code — including revisions to sanctions and policy changes — shall be authorized by the Grand Board of Directors. Adjustments to fines established under this Code may only be enacted by the Grand Chapter.

Zero-Tolerance on Pledging

Kappa has expressly prohibited pledging and dispensed with the practice of having pledges or protracted probationary periods since its founding. This opposition has been continuous and is explicit in a declaration first published in 1949.

The Membership Training Academy (MTA)

Kappa has established a structured membership intake program called The Membership Training Academy (MTA), which provides a disciplined and systematic process for recruiting, evaluating, and initiating members. The MTA is designed to attract and retain individuals who meet high academic, moral, social, civic, ethical, and character-based standards.

The official MTA process is the only approved pathway to membership. There is no alternative, unofficial, or supplemental process that is sanctioned by the Fraternity.

What Is Hazing?

Hazing includes roughhousing, physical challenges, antisocial acts, or any unlawful behavior used as conditions for membership. Any act or situation that recklessly or intentionally endangers the mental or physical health or safety of a person — or which willfully destroys property for the purpose of initiation — constitutes hazing.

Code of Silence = Participation

A member who is present during hazing activity, or who knew it was occurring and failed to stop or report it, faces the same Code liability as the member who actively participated. Silence is not neutrality — it is complicity.

▶ Scenario — Intake

Brother A is approached by fellow chapter members who suggest organizing an unofficial "get-to-know-you period" for new candidates involving late-night physical exercise and sleep deprivation before initiation. They frame it as tradition.

Under the Code, what is the correct response?

Brother A must refuse and immediately report this to the Province Polemarch. This describes hazing — any physical challenges or conditions imposed outside the MTA process are expressly prohibited, regardless of how they are framed. "Tradition" is not a defense.
Underground Pledging

"Wayward members of Kappa who do not subscribe to our motto of Achievement have elected, in open defiance of the rules of Kappa, to conduct underground pledging and engage in acts of hazing. Such members' criminal behavior is repugnant to all for which Kappa stands." — Code of Conduct

Membership Bar

Kappa also bars from membership any individual who willingly participates in underground pledging. This applies to prospective members and Candidates who choose to participate rather than report the activity.

Legal Framework

Texas Hazing Law — Chapter 37

Texas Education Code Chapter 37 establishes criminal liability for individuals and organizations that engage in, permit, or fail to report hazing. Understanding this statute is not optional — it defines conduct that can result in misdemeanor or felony charges, organizational fines reaching tens of thousands of dollars, and permanent reputational harm to the Fraternity.

This Is Criminal Law. The Code of Conduct is a fraternal disciplinary system. Texas Chapter 37 is a criminal statute. A single hazing incident can trigger both simultaneously — along with university discipline and civil lawsuits. None of these proceedings block or excuse the others.

§ 37.151 — Definitions

Texas defines hazing broadly. The statute reaches any intentional, knowing, or reckless act — occurring on or off campus — committed against a student for the purpose of pledging, initiation, affiliation, holding office, or maintaining membership in an organization.

The term "organization" is equally broad. It includes fraternities, sororities, associations, corporations, orders, societies, corps, clubs, student government bodies, bands, academic or athletic teams, cheerleading or dance teams, service groups, social groups, and similar organizations whose members are primarily students.

Conduct the Statute Reaches

Physical

Whipping, beating, striking, branding, electronic shocking, placing harmful substances on the body, or similar conduct

Physiological

Sleep deprivation, exposure to the elements, confinement in a small space, calisthenics, activities creating an unreasonable risk of harm

Health & Safety

Activities adversely affecting mental or physical health or safety, forced or dangerous consumption of food, liquids, alcohol, or drugs

Compelled Crimes

Conduct that induces or requires a student to perform a duty or task involving a violation of the Penal Code

Physical contact is not required. Sleep deprivation, psychological intimidation, forced consumption, and unauthorized confinement all qualify as hazing under the statute — even when no one is touched.

§ 37.152 — Personal Hazing Offense

Individual criminal liability attaches to a person who:

  • Engages in hazing;
  • Solicits, encourages, directs, aids, or attempts to aid another person in hazing;
  • Recklessly permits hazing to occur; or
  • Has firsthand knowledge of a planned or completed hazing incident and knowingly fails to report that knowledge in writing to the appropriate institutional official.

Penalty Escalation

Class B
No serious bodily injury

Misdemeanor. Applies to most hazing incidents that do not produce serious physical harm.

Class A / State Jail
Serious bodily injury

The offense level increases when hazing causes serious bodily injury to any person.

State Jail Felony
Death

Hazing that causes the death of another person is a state jail felony — a conviction that permanently marks a member's record.

Liability is not limited to the person who physically performs the act. A member who encourages, assists, attempts to assist, or recklessly permits hazing faces the same criminal exposure as the person who carried it out.

§ 37.153 — Organization Hazing Offense

Texas separately punishes the organization when it:

  • Condones or encourages hazing; or
  • An officer, combination of members, pledges, or alumni commits or assists in hazing.
Outcome of HazingOrganizational Fine
No personal injury or property damage$5,000 – $10,000
Personal injury, property damage, or other lossAt least $5,000 and up to twice the loss or expenses incurred
Alumni participation triggers organizational liability. The statute expressly references officers, members, pledges, and alumni. An alumni-planned "tradition" that harms a candidate creates criminal exposure for the chapter itself.

§ 37.154 — Consent Is Not a Defense

This is the provision members most often misunderstand. Texas law expressly provides:

"It is not a defense to prosecution under this subchapter that the person against whom the hazing was directed consented to or acquiesced in the hazing activity."

None of the following arguments removes legal liability under Chapter 37:

  • "He agreed to do it."
  • "Nobody forced him."
  • "He volunteered."
  • "Everyone went through it."
  • "He could have left."
  • "The candidate asked to participate."
  • "It's always been done this way."

§ 37.155 — Immunity for Reporting

Texas law creates an incentive to report. A person who voluntarily reports a specific hazing incident involving a student may receive immunity from civil and criminal liability arising from that incident — but only when both conditions are met:

1
Report before contact

The report must be made before the person is contacted by the institution or law enforcement about the incident, or otherwise becomes included in the investigation.

2
Cooperate in good faith

The person must cooperate in good faith throughout any institutional or law enforcement investigation and any resulting judicial proceedings.

Immunity does not apply when: the report covers the person's own hazing, or the report is made in bad faith or with malice.

§ 37.156 — Other Criminal Laws Still Apply

Chapter 37 does not replace other criminal statutes. A single hazing incident can generate multiple independent legal consequences running simultaneously:

1
Fraternity Code of Conduct

Suspension, expulsion, barment, or organizational sanctions under the Code

2
University Discipline

Student code proceedings, suspension, or loss of recognized chapter status

3
Texas Chapter 37 Charges

Criminal hazing offense — Class B misdemeanor through state jail felony

4
Other Criminal Charges

Assault, unlawful restraint, controlled substance or alcohol offenses under the Penal Code

5
Civil Liability

Tort claims by the injured party — potentially for substantial damages

§ 37.157 — Reporting by Medical Authorities

A doctor or other medical practitioner who treats a student who may have been subjected to hazing activities is addressed under § 37.157. This provision creates an independent pathway through which serious hazing incidents come to institutional or governmental attention — even when no member or candidate reports the incident directly. Medical treatment of a hazing victim is not confidential in the way that protects the incident from disclosure.

What This Means for Members

Texas law supports a prohibition considerably broader than "no physical hazing." The following categories of conduct are prohibited by both the Fraternity Code and Texas Chapter 37:

Physical

Striking, branding, shocking, or similar contact

Psychological

Intimidation, humiliation, threats, or coercion

Physiological

Sleep deprivation, excessive exercise, confinement, exposure

Compelled Consumption

Forced ingestion of food, liquids, alcohol, or substances

Encouragement

Assisting, directing, or facilitating another's hazing

Knowing Permission

Witnessing or knowing about hazing and failing to stop or report it

Consent, willingness, tradition, seniority, prior practice, and a candidate's failure to object do not authorize hazing and do not constitute a legal defense.
▶ Scenario — Texas Law

The night before initiation, three alumni Brothers organize a "tradition" at an off-campus location where candidates must perform two hours of calisthenics and are not permitted to leave. One candidate is taken to the hospital afterward with heat exhaustion. A chapter member who attended says nothing to anyone because he did not physically participate. The next day, the attending physician contacts university administration.

Alumni exposure (§ 37.153): All three alumni face personal criminal charges under § 37.152 and trigger organizational liability under § 37.153 — the statute expressly covers alumni participants.

Chapter member (§ 37.152): The attending member who said nothing faces criminal liability for recklessly permitting hazing to occur — his physical non-participation is not a defense.

Reporting immunity (§ 37.155): Once the university contacted administration, any member hoping to claim immunity lost that window. Immunity required reporting before being contacted.

Medical disclosure (§ 37.157): The physician's contact with university administration creates an independent investigative trigger — the incident now enters the institutional process regardless of what any member reports or withholds.

Concurrent consequences: This one event activates five independent tracks simultaneously — Fraternity Code, university discipline, Texas § 37 charges, potential assault charges, and civil liability by the injured candidate.

Legal Framework

Texas Law — Conduct & Financial Accountability

Beyond Chapter 37's hazing provisions, multiple Texas Penal Code statutes directly govern the kinds of conduct that arise in fraternal organizations — physical threats, harassment, electronic misconduct, fraud, and misappropriation of funds. A member need not be criminally charged for these statutes to be relevant: conduct that constitutes or reasonably appears to constitute criminal activity is simultaneously a Code violation.

Code proceedings are independent of criminal proceedings. The organization may investigate and impose sanctions regardless of whether a district attorney prosecutes, a case is dismissed, or a member is acquitted. An acquittal in criminal court is not a defense to a Code finding.

Relevant Texas Penal Code Offenses

Aggravated Assault § 22.02 Assault involving serious bodily injury or use/exhibition of a deadly weapon
Deadly Conduct § 22.05 Recklessly placing another in imminent danger of serious bodily injury; certain firearm conduct
Terroristic Threat § 22.07 Threatening violence against a person or property with intent to place someone in fear of imminent serious bodily injury or to disrupt certain places or services
Harassment § 42.07 Communications or conduct undertaken with intent to harass, annoy, alarm, abuse, torment, or embarrass; expressly covers electronic communications
Stalking § 42.072 Repeated conduct directed at a specific person satisfying the statute's threat, fear, and reasonable-person requirements
Online Impersonation § 33.07 Using another person's name or persona online without consent with intent to harm, defraud, intimidate, or threaten; covers deceptive electronic communications
Theft § 31.03 Unlawful appropriation of property with intent to deprive the owner of it

Social Media & Online Communications

Electronic conduct deserves its own category — not simply a footnote under harassment. Texas Penal Code § 33.07 (Online Impersonation) makes it an offense to use another person's name or persona online without consent and with intent to harm, defraud, intimidate, or threaten. Deceptive emails, texts, instant messages, and similar communications are separately covered. Electronic conduct can also intersect with § 42.07 (Harassment) and § 42.072 (Stalking).

The following conduct is prohibited by this Code whether or not it rises to the level of a criminal offense:

  • Threatening or intimidating posts, messages, or communications directed at any person;
  • Repeated unwanted electronic communications;
  • Cyberstalking;
  • Impersonating another member, officer, or candidate;
  • Creating accounts purporting to represent another person;
  • Publishing communications designed to appear authorized by another person;
  • Unauthorized use of organizational accounts or platforms;
  • Unauthorized disclosure of confidential organizational information;
  • Misuse of member personal information;
  • Fraudulent fundraising through social media; and
  • Representing personal statements as official organizational positions.

Financial Integrity & Organizational Property

Financial misconduct is broader than fraud. Any person who holds or controls organizational money, assets, or accounts — including elected officers, treasurers, committee chairs, event coordinators, and members with signatory authority — operates as a fiduciary. Texas Penal Code § 32.45 reaches misapplication of entrusted funds regardless of whether a formal fraud was intended; creating a substantial risk of loss is sufficient.

The intent-to-repay defense does not work. A treasurer who uses chapter funds for a personal expense "planning to repay next month" has already created a substantial risk of loss at the moment of the act. That is enough for § 32.45 exposure — and a simultaneous Code violation.

Prohibited financial conduct includes, but is not limited to:

  • Theft or embezzlement of organizational funds or property;
  • Misapplication of entrusted funds or property;
  • Forgery or falsification of financial records, receipts, or reimbursement claims;
  • Unauthorized transactions or expenditures;
  • Misuse of chapter credit or debit cards;
  • Diversion of donations or fundraising proceeds;
  • Unauthorized fundraising conducted in the organization's name;
  • Misuse of organizational property for personal benefit;
  • Concealment of transactions from officers or members entitled to review; and
  • Unauthorized access to financial accounts.

Identity-related financial misconduct may also implicate § 32.51 when a member's identifying information — including account numbers or electronic credentials — is obtained, transferred, or used without consent with intent to harm or defraud.

Compliance With Law — Recommended Framework

Rather than reproducing the Texas Penal Code inside a Code of Conduct, a well-drafted policy uses a bridging provision. The following language represents the standard approach for an organizational conduct manual:

Compliance With Law

Members shall comply with applicable federal, state, and local laws. Conduct that constitutes, or reasonably appears to constitute, criminal activity may also constitute a violation of this Code of Conduct regardless of whether the member is arrested, charged, prosecuted, convicted, or otherwise subjected to criminal proceedings.

Prohibited conduct includes, but is not limited to, assault, threats, harassment, stalking, intimidation, theft, fraud, forgery, financial misconduct, misapplication of entrusted property, identity-related offenses, online impersonation, unlawful electronic communications, property offenses, retaliation, and other conduct prohibited by applicable law.

Organizational disciplinary proceedings are separate from criminal proceedings. The organization may investigate and address conduct under its governing documents and applicable policies based upon the standards and procedures established by the organization.

Why this matters: This provision means the organization is never waiting on a DA. A member whose criminal case is dismissed on a technicality, whose prosecution is declined, or who is acquitted remains subject to the Code — because the standard of proof and the purpose of the proceedings are different.

▶ Scenario — Financial Conduct

The chapter Keeper of Exchequer uses the chapter's debit card to pay $340 in personal expenses over three months. He keeps a handwritten IOU in his desk and says he planned to repay the chapter at year-end. A member reviewing the chapter bank statement notices unfamiliar transactions and reports them to the Polemarch.

Texas Penal Code § 32.45: The Keeper created a substantial risk of loss to the chapter at the moment of each transaction — the intent to repay does not negate that exposure. Depending on the cumulative amount, this may constitute a Class A misdemeanor or felony.

Possible § 32.31 (Card Abuse): Unauthorized use of an organizational debit card can independently trigger credit/debit card abuse liability.

Code of Conduct: Regardless of whether criminal charges are filed, this conduct constitutes a financial misconduct violation — the unauthorized use of chapter funds for personal benefit. The Code proceeding does not wait on a prosecutor's decision.

Officer liability: Any officer who knew of the transactions and failed to report or act may face a separate Collusion charge under the Code for inaction with knowledge of a planned or ongoing violation.

Key Definitions

Precision matters. These definitions determine who is covered by each provision, what protections apply, and what sanctions are available.

⏱ 12 min📚 Terminology
Learning Objectives

After this module, you will be able to: (1) define the core terms used throughout the Code; (2) distinguish between Applicant, Candidate, Pre-Initiation Participant, and Member; (3) identify what constitutes Fraternity Property.

Understanding the Code begins with precision. Misidentifying a person's status can affect how a complaint is filed, what sanctions apply, and what procedural rights are available. These definitions are not suggestions.

Member Status Definitions

Applicant
An individual who has applied for the Kappa Alpha Psi membership intake process but has not yet received a bid. This person has not yet formally entered the process and has limited Code coverage.
Candidate
An individual who has received a bid and is actively participating in the Membership Training Academy. A Candidate is under the full jurisdiction of the Code of Conduct.
Pre-Initiation Participant
An individual who has successfully completed the MTA process and is awaiting initiation into full membership. Distinct from both Candidate and Member status — each has different procedural rights.
Member
An initiated brother of Kappa Alpha Psi. Bound by all provisions of the Code regardless of chapter, province, or alumni status. Membership does not expire; neither does Code obligation.

Property & Key Term Definitions

Fraternity Property
Any property owned, leased, or used by Kappa Alpha Psi — including real estate, ritual materials, financial instruments, records, documents, and digital assets. Broader than it may seem.
Hazing
Any act or situation which recklessly or intentionally endangers the mental or physical health or safety of a person, or which willfully destroys or removes public or private property for the purpose of initiation or affiliation with the Fraternity.
Province Polemarch
The senior elected officer of a Kappa province (regional district). The Province Polemarch plays a central role in administering the Code and has authority to impose sanctions within Code-established ranges.
Investigating Officer (IO)
An impartial member appointed by the Province Polemarch to investigate a complaint, gather evidence, and prepare a written report. The IO does not determine guilt — that is the Hearing Officer's role.

▶ Scenario — Social Media

"Brother Marcus posted a series of inflammatory comments on social media targeting a rival chapter member and making statements that brought public embarrassment to the Kappa Alpha Psi brand."

What level violation does this represent, and which specific provision applies?

This is a Level 1 violation under Section I — Conduct Unbecoming. Digital platforms and social media are explicitly covered. The fact that the comments targeted another individual also implicates potential civil liability for the chapter.

Section II — Hazing & Pledging Prohibition

Section II encompasses the full range of hazing and pledging prohibitions. Any act that endangers mental or physical health, or any unofficial membership conditioning activity, falls under this section. Critically, members who knew or should have known about hazing and failed to act are treated as participants.

What counts as an "act of hazing"?

Hazing includes but is not limited to: forced physical exercise, sleep deprivation, exposure to extreme weather conditions, forced consumption of any substance, paddling, striking, or any other physical act that could cause injury. It also includes psychological hazing — forced humiliation, verbal abuse, threats, and isolation.

Does intent matter?

No. The Code specifies both "recklessly or intentionally" — meaning even acts not intended to cause harm can constitute hazing if they create risk. The defense "we didn't mean for anyone to get hurt" is not a defense under the Code.

What if the candidate "consents"?

Consent is not a defense to hazing. The power imbalance inherent in the membership process means that a candidate's apparent "consent" is legally and ethically coerced. The Code treats all such conditioning as prohibited regardless of the candidate's stated willingness.

Sanctions & Fines

The Code establishes a tiered sanction framework. Consequences are matched to the severity of the violation — and differ based on the respondent's member status.

Who Imposes Sanctions?

The Province Polemarch receives the Hearing Officer's report and recommendation, then issues the final sanction. The Province Polemarch has discretion within the ranges established by the Code but may not impose sanctions outside those ranges.

Financial sanctions are imposed in addition to, not instead of, other sanctions. Unpaid fines may be referred for collection and can affect a member's good standing status.

Injunctive Relief

When immediate action is necessary to protect someone from harm, the Province Polemarch may impose interim restrictions before any formal hearing. These interim measures are temporary and do not constitute a finding of guilt.

Filing a Complaint

The Rules of Procedure ensure that every accused member receives a fair process. These rules protect both the integrity of the Fraternity and the rights of every respondent.

⏱ 22 min📝 Procedure
Learning Objectives

After this module, you will be able to: (1) describe how a complaint is properly initiated; (2) explain the role of the Investigating Officer; (3) identify the required contents of a complaint and the timeline for response.

Commencement of Action

A Code of Conduct proceeding commences when a written complaint is filed with the Province Polemarch. Any member, Candidate, Pre-Initiation Participant, or officer may file. The complaint must be submitted within a reasonable time of the alleged violation.

  1. File a written complaint with the Province Polemarch. Include all required elements (name of complainant, respondent, factual description, specific Code section(s), date/time/location).
  2. Complaint is served on the respondent by the Province Polemarch.
  3. Province Polemarch appoints an Investigating Officer (IO) — an impartial member with no personal interest in the matter.
  4. Respondent files an Answer within 10 days of receiving the complaint. Failure to answer is treated as an admission of the facts alleged.
  5. IO investigates — gathers evidence, interviews witnesses, prepares written report. Does not determine guilt.
  6. Hearing is scheduled — respondent receives written notice at least 10 days in advance.
Failure to Answer = Admission

The respondent has exactly 10 days from receipt of the complaint to file a written Answer. Failure to file is treated as an admission of the facts alleged. Proceedings continue in the respondent's absence.

▶ Scenario — No Answer Filed

"Brother D received a formal complaint on September 1. He decides to wait and see what happens, filing nothing by September 11."

What is the procedural consequence?

Brother D's failure to file an Answer within 10 days is treated as an admission of the facts alleged in the complaint. The Province Polemarch may proceed with the hearing and impose sanctions without Brother D's participation.

Hearing, Appeals & Special Provisions

The hearing process through the Province Polemarch's sanction, the appeals pathway, and special provisions including injunctive relief.

⏱ 20 min🏛️ Due Process
Learning Objectives

After this module, you will be able to: (1) explain the hearing process from attendance through the Hearing Officer's decision; (2) describe the appeals process including appeals as of right and discretionary appeals; (3) identify special provisions including financial sanctions and injunctive relief.

The Hearing

Both the complainant and the respondent have the right to attend the hearing. Each party may present evidence and call witnesses. Respondents may be represented by a brother member in good standing (not an attorney in a professional capacity) or appear pro se.

The Hearing Officer issues a written decision based on the preponderance of the evidence standard — meaning it is more likely than not that the violation occurred. This is a civil standard, not the criminal "beyond a reasonable doubt" standard.

Standard of Proof

Preponderance of Evidence

More likely than not that the violation occurred. Lower than the criminal standard — but still requires a reasoned, evidence-based conclusion.

Representation

Member in Good Standing

Respondents may bring a brother member as a representative. Licensed attorneys may not appear in a professional legal capacity in the hearing.

Appeals

Appeal as of Right (Section L)

A respondent who receives a sanction has an automatic right to appeal by filing a written notice within 10 days of receiving the sanction. The appeal goes to the next level of Fraternity authority. An appeal as of right must allege a specific procedural error or that the sanction is disproportionate.

Discretionary Appeal (Section M)

After exhausting the appeal as of right, a respondent may seek a discretionary appeal to the Grand Board of Directors. This is not automatic — it is granted at the Grand Board's discretion when the matter raises a significant question about the Code's interpretation or application.

▶ Scenario — Province Polemarch Discretion

"The Hearing Officer recommends a $400 fine for a Level 1 violation. The Province Polemarch believes the circumstances warrant the maximum penalty."

May the Province Polemarch impose a $500 fine instead?

Yes. The Province Polemarch has discretion within Code-established ranges. A $500 fine is within the Level 1 Member range (maximum $500), so this modification is permissible. The Province Polemarch may adopt, modify, or reject the Hearing Officer's recommendation within those ranges.
