The Context — Indiana University, 1910–11

The early 20th century was a time of profound racial segregation — including on college campuses. Black students at Indiana University faced isolation, exclusion from campus organizations, and a social environment that offered them little sense of belonging or collective purpose.

It was in this climate that ten young men — visionaries, scholars, and leaders — chose not to accept their circumstances. Instead, they created something. On January 5, 1911, in Bloomington, Indiana, they founded what would become one of the most storied fraternities in American history.

"We were not just founding a fraternity. We were asserting that Black men of scholarship and ambition belonged — and that we would build the institution to prove it."

The Spirit of 1911

The Ten Founders

Elder Watson Diggs (1883–1947) — Polemarch & Primary Founder

Considered the principal founder of Kappa Alpha Psi, Elder Watson Diggs was born in Christian County, Kentucky in 1883. A scholar, educator, and man of extraordinary vision, Diggs was the driving force behind the fraternity's formation. He served as the first Grand Polemarch — and served six consecutive terms, the most of any Grand Polemarch. He was awarded the Fraternity's first Laurel Wreath. His phrase — "Achievement in every field of human endeavor" — became the fraternity's motto and still guides us today. The Coat of Arms was co-designed by Diggs, Armstrong, and Lee. The annual Elder Watson Diggs Award is among the fraternity's highest honors.

Byron Kenneth Armstrong (1892–1980)

Born in Westfield, Indiana, Byron Kenneth Armstrong was among the most academically distinguished of the Founders. He studied philosophy, mathematics, and sociology at Indiana University, then earned an M.A. from Columbia University and a Ph.D. from the University of Michigan. He held teaching positions in multiple states and served as an investigator for the U.S. Department of Labor during World War I. Armstrong was a co-designer of the Fraternity Coat of Arms alongside Diggs and Lee.

Ezra Dee Alexander (1892–1971)

Born in Bloomington, Indiana, Ezra Dee Alexander graduated from Bloomington High School in 1910, then from Indiana University in 1917, and earned his M.D. from Indiana University Medical School in 1919. He practiced medicine in Indianapolis and served on the Grand Board of Directors, bringing the disciplined precision of a physician to fraternity leadership. His life embodied the founding ideal: academic achievement in service of community.

Henry Tourner Asher (1890–1963)

Henry Tourner Asher was a founding brother whose participation in the founding meetings of January 1911 helped shape the fraternity's early direction. His contributions are documented in the official fraternity records and in the Tribute to Founders Ceremony. His life reflects the collective courage of men who dared to build an institution of Brotherhood at a time when the world offered them little welcome.

Marcus Peter Blakemore (1889–1959)

Born in Franklin, Indiana, Marcus Peter Blakemore was a man of deep religious conviction whose faith grounded his commitment to Brotherhood. Affectionately known as "Blakie" among his fraternity brothers, he contributed to education and community throughout his life. His quiet devotion to the fraternity's ideals exemplifies the character the Founders hoped all members would embody.

Paul Waymond Caine (1890–1931)

Paul Waymond Caine was one of the youngest of the founding group and among those whose lives were cut shortest — passing at just 41 years of age. His presence at Indiana University and his commitment to the founding vision left a permanent mark on the fraternity. He is honored annually in the Tribute to Founders Ceremony. His story is a reminder that what we build together outlasts any individual life.

George Wesley Edmonds (1890–1962)

George Wesley Edmonds was a founding brother whose life and contributions are part of the official fraternity record. His participation in the founding meetings at Indiana University in 1911 made him one of the ten men who changed the course of Black Greek-letter history. He is honored in the Tribute to Founders Ceremony, which recognizes each Founder individually for their lasting legacy.

Guy Levis Grant (1891–1973)

Born in New Albany, Indiana, Guy Levis Grant became a dentist who practiced for over 50 years in Indianapolis. He was known for his charitable work and his dedication to preserving fraternity history. A co-designer of the Fraternity Coat of Arms alongside Diggs and Armstrong, Grant's impact is literally embedded in the symbol every Kappa wears. The fraternity's Guy Levis Grant Award Medallion bears his name.

Edward Giles Irvin (1893–1982)

Edward Giles Irvin was the youngest of the Ten Founders, born in 1893, and the last to pass — living to age 89. His long life meant he witnessed decades of the fraternity's growth, from a student organization at Indiana University to an international brotherhood. His presence among the Founders and his longevity made him a living bridge between the fraternity's origins and its modern era.

John Milton Lee (1890–1958)

John Milton Lee was a founding brother and, alongside Diggs and Armstrong, one of the three Founders who co-designed the Fraternity Coat of Arms — the official symbol every member carries. His contributions to the early organizational structure and identity of Kappa Alpha Psi helped give the fraternity its visual and symbolic language. His legacy lives in every badge worn by every brother initiated since 1911.

◆ Myth Buster

Myth: "The fraternity was always called Kappa Alpha Psi."

Truth: The fraternity was originally named Kappa Alpha Nu — a name that carried a racial slur embedded in it that was used to mock the founders. In 1915, the brothers voted to change the name to Kappa Alpha Psi, reclaiming their identity on their own terms. Knowing this history is part of understanding who we are and what our founders overcame.

What is MTA?

The Membership Training Academy (MTA) is Kappa Alpha Psi's official membership selection and orientation process for alumni chapters. It is a structured, five-step program that governs how the fraternity identifies, evaluates, selects, and orients new members.

MTA is not optional, not negotiable, and not something any chapter can modify on its own. The process exists to protect candidates, protect brothers, protect the chapter, and protect the integrity of the fraternity as a whole.

☠ Critical Notice

"Recreating your own MTA program is prohibited." — MTA 6th Edition Manual

No chapter, province, or group of brothers has the authority to create an alternative intake process. The five-step MTA is the only authorized path to membership in Kappa Alpha Psi for alumni chapters. Any deviation — any "tradition" or "old school" method that circumvents this process — is a violation of fraternity policy and potentially illegal.

The Five Steps of MTA

I

Chapter Certification

Before any intake activity can begin, the chapter must be certified by the Province and Grand Chapter. This includes verifying that the chapter is in good standing, that qualified officers are in place, and that all required forms (including UCOR-1 and ACOR-1) have been submitted and approved. No certification = no intake. This is non-negotiable.

II

Informational Meeting

The chapter hosts an open informational meeting for prospective candidates. This is where interested men learn what membership involves — the commitments, expectations, fees, and process. It is also where the chapter begins to assess prospective members. No invitation to membership is extended at this stage.

III

Membership Selection

The chapter votes on candidates through a secret ballot. To be selected, an alumni candidate must receive a 75% favorable vote of the chapter. Candidates must also hold a degree from a four-year accredited college or university. The selection process is confidential — results are not shared outside authorized channels.

IV

Preparing & Verifying Candidate Packets

Once selected, candidates assemble a formal packet of required documentation — academic credentials, health clearances, personal information, and financial arrangements. The chapter verifies all materials for accuracy and completeness before forwarding to the Province and Grand Chapter. Incomplete packets delay the process.

V

Orientation & Initiation

The final step includes a structured One Day Training Session (8 hours) followed by the formal initiation ceremony. The One Day Training Session is where candidates learn the essential knowledge they need before becoming brothers.

Preamble — The Fraternity’s Position

Kappa Alpha Psi’s opposition to hazing or unlawful or anti-social conduct of any kind has been continuous since its founding. The Fraternity has outlawed pledging and dispensed with the customary practice of having pledges prior to initiation into full membership.

The Fraternity seeks to: (1) remove any and all opportunities for pledging, hazing, antisocial and unlawful activity; and (2) promote in every phase of fraternity life only safe and wholesome activities consistent with the Code of Conduct of the colleges, universities, and jurisdictions where our chapters are chartered.

The Fraternity’s Membership Training Academy is the sole method for obtaining membership in the organization. It does not tolerate either acts of physical aggression by members or the willing submission to the same by prospective members or candidates. The Fraternity has not only outlawed pledging, but also bans from its membership those who submit to such a process.

What Is the MTA?

The Membership Training Academy (MTA) is Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity's formal post-initiation membership training program. The key word is post-initiation — MTA begins only after a brother has been formally initiated. It is not a pre-initiation requirement, and it is absolutely not a hazing program.

The MTA replaced earlier pledge and intake processes that had become associated with hazing. It was designed to give new members a structured, educational, and brotherhood-building experience — one grounded in the fraternity's values rather than in physical or emotional coercion.

☠ Myth Buster

Myth: "The MTA is just pledging with a different name."

Truth: Absolutely not. Pledging (and anything that resembles it) is prohibited by Kappa Alpha Psi. The MTA has no "line," no "pledge masters," and no hazing of any kind. It is an educational program with defined learning objectives, conducted by chapter leaders in a structured setting. If anyone tells you the MTA includes activities that demean, embarrass, or physically/emotionally harm you — that is hazing, and it is illegal and against fraternity policy.

The Four Objectives of the MTA

The MTA has four core objectives that guide everything it does:

01

Strengthen Retention

Help new brothers stay active and engaged in the fraternity. Dropout rates decrease when brothers feel prepared, connected, and valued from the beginning.

02

Foster Brotherly Love

Build genuine bonds of brotherhood among new members and between them and the wider chapter. Brotherhood is not given — it is cultivated through shared experience and mutual respect.

03

Develop Fraternity Knowledge

Ensure every brother has a thorough understanding of Kappa Alpha Psi's history, traditions, structure, and expectations — not just facts to recite, but values to live by.

04

Promote Service & Achievement

Connect new members to the fraternity's core purpose: Achievement in every field of human endeavor. MTA equips brothers to be contributors — to their chapter, their community, and their profession.

"The MTA is not designed to test how much you can endure — it is designed to equip you for how much you can achieve."

Kappa Alpha Psi Membership Standards

Understanding the MTA begins with understanding what Kappa Alpha Psi expects of its members — not just during training, but for life. The following standards are taken directly from Fraternity policy and define the character of membership in Kappa Alpha Psi.

Membership — A Lifelong Commitment

Membership is a solemn commitment. To this Fraternity, the maker of the commitment becomes synonymous with the commitment itself. Membership in Kappa Alpha Psi® is a lifelong dedication to the ideas and lofty purposes of Kappa Alpha Psi®, which considers for membership only those aspirants whose personal, social and academic qualifications are acceptable to both the College and Fraternity.

Expectations concerning maturity and dedication are made upon new members and established members alike.

Maturity — More Than an Age

Maturity is more than simply reaching a certain age. Maturity to us means acceptance of reality, the ability to sacrifice and set goals, consideration of others, incisive judgment, emotional balance, development of social skills, intellectual competence, and moral rectitude. We expect our members to be mature.

Quality Over Quantity

Kappa Alpha Psi® is not interested in the simple accumulation of members and chapters. Its emphasis, however, is in the quality and integrity of its membership. Membership quality manifests itself through an intangible element of brotherhood we call "that Good Old Kappa Spirit."

Dedication — Maturity in Action

Dedication means putting those characteristics of maturity into a lifelong program of action to advance the goals and purposes of Kappa Alpha Psi®. We expect our members to be dedicated.

But do not let personal goals supersede those of the Fraternity. A person who feels he would do the Fraternity a favor by joining is not worthy of membership. No one who holds himself superior to the Brotherhood of Kappa Alpha Psi® is worthy of this distinction.

Acceptable Qualities of a Kappa Alpha Psi Member

Acceptable qualities of Kappa Alpha Psi® members include:

  • The disposition to live in close companionship with men of similar values.
  • The wisdom to abide by the will of the majority without sacrificing individuality.
  • The discretion to refrain from destructive criticism while seeking to remove the causes of such criticism.
  • The acceptance of responsibility for any effort that moves the group forward.
  • The desire to strive for, and ultimately achieve, excellence in everything you do.
  • The commitment to train for leadership.
  • The resolution to give lifelong financial support and commitment to the maintenance and development of the Fraternity's programs and purposes.
Academic Eligibility — Recognized Accrediting Agencies

Kappa Alpha Psi® requires that members attend or have completed degree requirements from a college or university accredited by one of the following recognized accrediting agencies:

  • Middle States Association of Colleges and Schools
  • North Central Association of Colleges and Schools (HLC)
  • Western Association of Schools and Colleges
  • Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities
  • Southern Association of Colleges and Schools
  • New England Association of Schools and Colleges

Academic eligibility is a prerequisite for membership — not a formality. Kappa Alpha Psi is a college-based fraternity, and the academic credential is foundational to the character of membership.

MTA Orientation & Onboarding Process

The MTA does not begin on the day of initiation. It is a structured, multi-phase process that starts before a candidate is ever accepted — and continues through post-initiation recognition. As a new brother, you have already moved through this process. Understanding each phase helps you see the full arc of what brought you here.

01

Pre-Intake & Approval

The chapter submits documentation to participate in MTA. Candidates complete applications, interviews, and must have all materials submitted via KappaOrg.com. Fraternity and (for undergraduates) university approvals are secured. Risk management documents, membership intake agreements, and anti-hazing materials are signed.

02

Formal Informational Meeting

A structured, professional meeting is held for prospective members. The chapter presents Kappa Alpha Psi, explains the intake process, and covers the fraternity's stance on hazing. Refund policies and next steps are communicated clearly.

03

Administrative & Insurance Meetings

The chapter conducts an administrative meeting covering the Code of Conduct, Keeping Kappa Safe & Compliance, risk management documents, indemnification agreements, and fees. An event insurance checklist is completed per IHQ requirements.

04

Kappa Kore Meetings (6 Meetings)

The core educational program — six meetings over approximately four weeks, covering everything from fraternity fundamentals to local chapter history. These must be completed before the Cluster Weekend. Three quizzes are administered: after Meetings 1–2, after Meetings 3–4, and after Meetings 5–6.

05

Kappa Cluster Initiation Weekend

Candidates who have completed the Kappa Kore curriculum and passed their assessments are initiated at a Province-hosted Cluster Weekend. Dates and locations are communicated by the chapter advisor. Formal initiation into the Bond happens here — not before.

06

Post-Initiation Requirements & Recognition

Applicants will be nervous. Interviewers shall do what they can to minimize nervousness so that applicants can think and respond appropriately — the goal is to evaluate the candidate, not intimidate them.

Member Conduct During the Interview & Evaluation Process

No member of Kappa Alpha Psi holds investigative authority over a candidate during the MTA process. Members shall not conduct personal investigations into a candidate’s background, gather outside information about a candidate, or introduce or share such findings — formally or informally — at any point during the MTA. This prohibition applies to all members, regardless of chapter position or seniority.

Introducing or sharing investigative findings about a candidate carries serious consequences at multiple levels:

  • Legal exposure — unauthorized background reporting or dissemination of unverified personal information can constitute defamation, exposing both the individual member and the chapter to civil liability.
  • Discrimination liability — information pertaining to protected classes (race, religion, disability, national origin, etc.) introduced into a membership process can form the basis of a discrimination lawsuit against the Fraternity.
  • Libel & slander — written or spoken false statements of fact about a candidate shared to chapter members constitute libel and slander, respectively, under both civil and fraternal law.

These actions are recognized under the Kappa Alpha Psi Code of Conduct as conduct unbecoming of a member — a sanctionable offense that may result in suspension, expulsion, or referral to the Grand Board of Directors. If you become aware of concerning information about a candidate through official channels, the appropriate path is to contact the Chapter Polemarch or Southwest Province leadership — not to raise it within the MTA process.

⚠ Legal & Ethical Boundary

The MTA is an educational program — not an investigative tribunal. Your role as a member is to teach, model, and support. If a member conducts or distributes unauthorized candidate research, that member — and potentially the chapter — assumes personal legal liability. This is not a grey area.

Pre-Acceptance Steps

The following eight steps govern the chapter’s process before any candidate is conditionally accepted. Each step carries specific timing requirements that protect the integrity of the review and selection process.

Province Cluster Weekend

The time, date, and location of the Province Cluster Weekend are set by the Province. Chapter participation is required. All subsequent timelines in the MTA process flow from this anchor event.

Formal Informational Meeting (FIM)

The FIM must be held within two (2) weeks of the Province Polemarch’s signature authorizing the MTA process. The meeting is limited to three (3) hours maximum and must be conducted using the IHQ-approved MTA PowerPoint presentation. No substitutions or unauthorized materials may be used.

3

Electronic Application Packet

The electronic application packet is distributed to prospective candidates fourteen (14) days after the Formal Informational Meeting. All applications are submitted electronically through KappaOrg.com.

Application Review & Grading

All submitted applications must be reviewed and graded within two (2) days of the application deadline. Only brothers in Good Standing who are also MTA-Certified may participate in the review and grading process.

5

Email Notification of Interview Date

Candidates who meet the threshold for advancement must receive written email notification of their interview date and time within five (5) days of the completion of the application review. Notification must be sent through official chapter communication channels.

6

Conditional Acceptance

Acceptance is determined by a 75% secret ballot of the chapter’s voting-eligible members in Good Standing. 7

Province & IHQ MTA Fees

Blazer Measurements

Core Alcohol & Drug Rules

Assault & Battery

Firearms, Explosive or Incendiary Devices

The chapter must comply with all federal, state, provincial, local laws and campus policy relating to firearms or explosive or incendiary devices.

Firearms and explosive or incendiary devices are prohibited from the chapter facility and at all Fraternity activities and events — without exception.

Fraud

Fraud against Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc. — including undergraduate and alumni chapters — is prohibited. Fraud is defined as the use of deception with the intention of obtaining an advantage, avoiding an obligation, or causing loss to another party.

Examples include but are not limited to: deception, bribery, forgery, extortion, corruption, theft, conspiracy, embezzlement, misappropriation, false representation, concealment of material facts, and collusion.

Hazing — Policy Definition

“Hazing” means any intentional, knowing, or reckless act committed by a person — whether individually or in concert — against any individual or group, regardless of affiliation, whether committed on chapter or member property, for the purpose of intake, joining, pledging, initiating, admitting, affiliating, or retaining membership in an organization, that causes an individual to do any of the following regardless of willingness to participate:

  1. Be coerced to violate federal, state, provincial, local law, or organizational policy.
  2. Be coerced to consume any food, liquid, alcoholic liquid, drug, or other substance in any non-customary manner which subjects the individual to a substantial risk of emotional or physical harm — including sickness, vomiting, intoxication, or unconsciousness.
  3. Endure brutality of a physical nature, including but not limited to whipping, beating, paddling, branding, dangerous physical activity, or exposure to elements — or endure threats of such conduct resulting in mental or physical harm.
  4. Endure brutality of a mental nature, including but not limited to activity adversely affecting mental health or dignity, sleep deprivation, exclusion from social contact, or conduct that could result in extreme embarrassment — or endure threats of such conduct resulting in mental or physical harm.
  5. Endure any other activity which adversely affects the health and safety of an individual — including but not limited to disruption of academic performance or class attendance, required designated driving programs, line-ups, calisthenics, or personal, physical, or financial servitude.
Housing Requirements

  • $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 General Aggregate for General Liability;
  • $1,000,000 per occurrence and in the aggregate for Liquor Liability, if applicable;
  • Statutory workers’ compensation, if applicable.

Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc. and the chapter (if not the owner) must be listed as additional insured on a primary basis. A current certificate of insurance must be on file with the Office of the Executive Director at International Headquarters.

Retaliation

Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc. prohibits retaliation against any individual — members and non-members — for reporting, inquiring, or cooperating with a report related to a violation of the Risk Management Policy.

Retaliation is any action, statement, or behavior designed to punish an individual for filing a report, cooperating with an investigation, seeking guidance regarding a concern, or to deter one from taking such action.

Sexual Misconduct

The chapter must comply with all federal, state, provincial, and local laws related to sexual misconduct — including but not limited to consent, sexual violence, sexual harassment, domestic violence, dating violence, stalking, and sexual exploitation.

The employment or use of strippers, exotic dancers, or similar — whether professional or amateur — at any Fraternity activity or event is prohibited.

What Is Expected of a Kappa?

Membership in Kappa Alpha Psi is not passive. It is active, ongoing, and demanding — in the best sense of the word. The fraternity identifies six core membership expectations that every brother is expected to embody. These are not suggestions. They are the standard.

01

Disposition

Approach your membership — and your life — with the right attitude. A positive, mature, and committed disposition makes you effective as a brother, a leader, and a man. Your attitude shapes how you represent Kappa in every room you enter.

02

Wisdom

Use sound judgment. Know when to speak and when to listen. Make decisions that reflect your values and the values of the fraternity. Wisdom is not just knowledge — it is knowledge applied with discernment and maturity.

03

Discretion

As a Kappa, you will be trusted with the affairs, traditions, and internal matters of the fraternity. Handle that trust with care. Not everything is for public consumption. Discretion is how you protect the integrity of the Brotherhood.

04

Acceptance

Brotherhood is broader than your line, your class, or your chapter. Accept your brothers across lines of politics, background, age, and perspective. A house divided against itself cannot stand. Kappa is strongest when we are unified.

05

Desire

You must want to be Kappa — not just have been initiated. Desire fuels active participation, service, leadership, and growth. A brother who attends one meeting a year is technically a member. A brother who desires to be Kappa shows up.

06

Commitment

Membership is for life. Financial obligations, chapter participation, service, professional development — these are commitments you made when you were initiated. Commitment is what separates a member on paper from a Kappa in practice.

◆ Myth Buster

Myth: "As long as I pay my dues, I'm doing my part."

Truth: Dues are the floor, not the ceiling. Kappa Alpha Psi expects brothers to be active, service-oriented, and engaged in chapter and community life. Financial standing is a minimum requirement — not a measure of good membership. Ask any chapter leader what they actually need from brothers, and the answer will never be "just your check."

The Governance Structure

Kappa Alpha Psi operates under a national governance structure that connects every chapter to the Grand Chapter. Understanding this structure helps you know who governs the fraternity, how decisions are made, and where your chapter fits in the larger organization.

I

Grand Chapter

The supreme governing body of Kappa Alpha Psi. The Grand Chapter is led by the Grand Polemarch — the national president of the fraternity. It is composed of all chapters and meets at the Grand Chapter Meeting (formerly called the Grand Conclave). The Grand Chapter sets policy, elects national officers, and governs the fraternity as a whole.

II

The Province System

Kappa Alpha Psi is divided into geographic Provinces. Each Province is led by a Provincial Polemarch and an Executive Committee. Provinces connect chapters regionally, provide oversight, coordinate programming, and serve as the governance layer between individual chapters and the Grand Chapter. DLA belongs to the Southwest Province.

III

Local Chapters

Individual chapters — both undergraduate and alumni — are the foundation of the fraternity. Each chapter is led by a Polemarch and governed by its own officers. Alumni chapters (like DLA) operate under the alumni chapter structure and report to their Province.

MTA Chain of Authority & Oversight

The Membership Training Academy operates within a defined chain of authority. Every role in this chain carries specific responsibilities — and specific limits. Understanding who holds what authority protects the integrity of the process, protects new members, and protects the chapter.

MTA Chairman — Training Development & Operations

The MTA Chairman is responsible for the development, delivery, and day-to-day operation of the MTA process. This includes curriculum preparation, scheduling, facilitator coordination, and ensuring all training activities align with IHQ guidelines.

Critical boundary: The MTA Chairman does not hold ultimate authority over the MTA process. His authority extends to training development and operational execution — not to chapter governance, member discipline, or decisions about the intake process itself. The MTA Chairman cannot:

  • “Hide” initiates from chapter leadership or provincial oversight
  • Instruct or pressure initiates to remain silent if asked about their experience by the Vice Polemarch, Polemarch, or Province Polemarch
  • Restrict access to the process by authorized observers or representatives
Vice Polemarch — Overseer of the MTA Process

The Vice Polemarch serves as the chapter-level overseer of the MTA process. He has the authority to intervene in the process at any time he deems it necessary — whether due to policy concerns, conduct issues, or the welfare of new members.

The Vice Polemarch may recommend removal of the MTA Chairman to the Polemarch, whereupon the matter goes before the chapter board for review and a final decision. His oversight role is continuous, not conditional — the MTA Chairman operates within the Vice Polemarch’s authority, not independently of it.

Chapter Polemarch — Overall Responsibility

The Chapter Polemarch holds overall responsibility for the MTA process at the chapter level. Approval to conduct MTA is obtained from the Province Polemarch — the Chapter Polemarch does not have unilateral authority to initiate or continue the MTA process without provincial sanction.

Removal of the MTA Chairman is placed directly in the Polemarch’s hands — acting in concert with the chapter board, or upon the Vice Polemarch’s recommendation, whereupon the board reviews findings and renders a decision.

The Chapter Polemarch — and any other chapter member — is required to report any violations of the MTA process, Code of Conduct violations, or conduct detrimental to the organization through the appropriate provincial channels.

Province Polemarch — First Level of Escalation

The Province Polemarch is the first level of contact for MTA issues involving Code of Conduct violations or conduct detrimental to the organization. He grants approval for a chapter to conduct MTA and retains authority to suspend, modify, or terminate a chapter’s MTA process.

The Province Polemarch may send or provide a representative of his choosing to observe and verify that a chapter is following proper MTA protocol. This may include, but is not limited to:

  • The Regional Administrative Representative
  • Province MTA Representatives
  • Any other officer or delegate serving in an observer role

Upon recognition of a Province representative, no interference shall occur. The Chapter Polemarch, MTA Chairman, and all chapter members must provide full access and cooperation. No chapter officer may obstruct, delay, or limit a province-authorized review or observation.

Membership Verification

When you encounter a man who identifies himself as a Kappa, it is your responsibility to verify that he is a member. Simply asking what chapter he is from is insufficient. If a man cannot engage in the recognition protocol or produce a card, be courteous — but do not recognize him as a member. Verifying membership is different from determining status: a suspended or expelled member may still recall the protocol or hold a card.

When you bring an unverified man into a group of brothers, you are "vouching" for him — your credibility is on the line.

Code of Conduct & Anti-Hazing

The Code of Conduct is not bureaucracy — it is the standard by which Kappa brothers are held accountable. And anti-hazing is not a policy preference — it is a legal, ethical, and fraternal imperative.

What is Hazing?

Hazing is defined as any action or situation that recklessly or intentionally endangers the mental or physical health or safety of a student or member for the purpose of initiation or admission into or affiliation with any organization.

This definition is intentionally broad — and it applies regardless of whether the person being hazed consents to it. You cannot consent to being hazed. Consent is not a defense.

⚠ Hazing is Illegal in Texas

In Texas, hazing is a criminal offense under the Texas Education Code § 37.152. It can result in criminal charges for individuals who engage in hazing, fail to report hazing, or encourage others to remain silent about hazing. Ignorance of the law is not a defense. Neither is "it's tradition" or "this is how it's always been done."

For Kappa Alpha Psi specifically, hazing results in chapter suspension, charter revocation, and individual expulsion from the fraternity. The consequences are permanent and career-affecting.

What Counts as Hazing?

Many brothers have mistaken ideas about what qualifies as hazing. The list below is not exhaustive — if something causes physical or emotional distress, embarrassment, or endangerment for the purpose of membership, it likely qualifies. Examples include:

Physical Hazing

Physical Activity & Abuse

Excessive exercise, sleep deprivation, paddling, hitting, forced exposure to cold or heat, forced eating or drinking, or any form of physical harm or discomfort imposed on candidates.

Psychological Hazing

Emotional Abuse

Humiliation, degradation, public shaming, screaming, threats, forced servitude, or any action designed to make candidates feel less than — regardless of whether physical harm occurs.

Activity Hazing

Forced Activities

Mandatory attendance at events not related to the MTA process, isolation from family or friends, financial exploitation, theft, or forced participation in activities unrelated to legitimate fraternal education.

Social Hazing

Ostracism & Control

Restricting candidates' movements, requiring them to carry items at all times, controlling their dress or appearance, or imposing "rules" not found in any official fraternity document.

◆ Myth Buster

Myth: "If the person doesn't complain, it's not hazing."

Truth: Silence is not consent. Many people who experience hazing do not report it because they fear retaliation, want to be liked, or believe it is a required part of joining. None of that makes hazing acceptable. If the activity would cause distress, embarrassment, or harm — it is hazing, regardless of whether anyone speaks up in the moment.

◆ Myth Buster

Myth: "Brothers who went through hazing turned out fine — it builds character."

Truth: This argument has been used to justify hazing for decades — and it has been accompanied by countless injuries, hospitalizations, and deaths. Hazing does not build character; it builds trauma and resentment. Brotherhood is built through genuine shared experience, mentorship, and service — not through suffering imposed by one person on another. The MTA exists precisely to provide the brotherhood-building experience without the harm.

What to Do If You Witness or Experience Hazing

  1. Remove yourself from the situation if it is safe to do so. Your safety comes first. You are not obligated to participate in hazing — ever.
  2. Report to the chapter Polemarch. If the hazing is occurring within a chapter context, the Polemarch is the first point of contact. If the Polemarch is involved, escalate immediately.
  3. Report to the Province. The Provincial Polemarch and Province Executive Committee are authorized to investigate and act on hazing allegations.
  4. Report to the Grand Chapter. The Grand Chapter has a designated process for receiving and investigating hazing reports. Contact information is available through the official KAP website.
  5. Report to law enforcement if a crime has occurred. If hazing has resulted in physical harm, contact local law enforcement. Texas law requires reporting of hazing in certain circumstances.

Scenarios — What Would You Do?

Scenario A

A new brother is told by several older members that he needs to "earn his place" by running errands for them every week, not speaking unless spoken to at chapter meetings, and always having water available for members who want it. The new brother thinks this is "just how it works."

Is this hazing? What should the new brother do?

Yes — this is hazing. Forced servitude, imposed silence, and arbitrary "rules" imposed on new members for the purpose of membership conditioning are all forms of hazing, regardless of whether physical harm occurs. The new brother should document what is happening, report it to the Polemarch (or Province if the Polemarch is involved), and know that he has every right to refuse these demands.

Scenario B

You overhear brothers discussing plans to have the new intake class come to a private location for "extra training" that is not part of the official MTA process. One brother says, "Don't worry — we just want to make sure they really know their stuff. Nothing bad is going to happen."

What are the red flags here? What is the right response?

Multiple red flags: (1) Unofficial, off-MTA-process activity involving candidates; (2) A private location not sanctioned by chapter leadership; (3) Reassurances that "nothing bad will happen" — a phrase almost always used when something bad is being planned. The right response is to not participate, report the plan to the Polemarch and Province immediately, and document everything. "I didn't do anything" is not sufficient — silence in the face of hazing can also carry liability.

  • Reports generated on personal software may contain errors, omissions, or inconsistencies that contradict the official record — and in a dispute, the official record prevails.
  • These controls protect the integrity of the MTA process, the chapter’s financial standing, and every brother involved from accusations of misappropriation or fraud.

☠ The Specific Problem This Module Addresses

Underground cadences — unauthorized chants that circulate orally through Greek culture — are one of the most persistent sources of organizational liability, embarrassment, and disciplinary action in Black Greek-letter organizations. They are passed down informally, adapted locally, and often recited publicly without awareness of their consequences. This module is not about policing culture for its own sake. It is about protecting you, protecting this chapter, and protecting the organization you chose to join.

What Is an "Underground" Cadence?

An underground cadence is any chant, call, stroll rhythm, or oral tradition that:

  • Was not published in the fraternity's official songbook or ritual literature
  • Originated in unauthorized intake processes, chapter subcultures, or local tradition
  • Has been modified from official material to include prohibited content
  • Circulates informally — passed from class to class, region to region — without IHQ sanction

The fact that a chant has been recited for twenty years does not make it authorized. The fact that older brothers taught it to you does not make it protected. The fact that everyone on the yard knows it does not make it appropriate. Longevity is not legitimacy.

Five Categories of Prohibited Conduct

Prohibited chants and cadences fall into five behavioral categories. Every underground cadence that creates organizational liability maps to at least one of these. Knowing the categories helps you evaluate content — not just recognize a specific chant.

Category 1 — Substance Use & Illicit Activity

Chants that glorify drug use, binge drinking, alcohol abuse, or illegal activity. These cadences associate the fraternity's name, call, or imagery with narcotics, intoxication, or criminal behavior — creating both reputational damage and potential legal exposure for brothers who recite them.

Common motifs: References to "smoking dope," excessive drinking, dealing substances, or framing intoxication as a fraternal lifestyle. One of the most widely circulated underground cadences in Kappa culture explicitly pairs the fraternity's call with references to drug use, sexual misconduct, and the phrase "til the casket close" — a lyric that has circulated in multiple regional variations for decades. That cadence is not Kappa. It is not authorized. It is prohibited under both the Code of Conduct and the fraternity's public representation policy.

Policy basis: The public decorum clause prohibits any cadence that promotes illegal substance use or alcohol abuse, regardless of how widely it has circulated.

Category 2 — Misogyny & Sexual Degradation

Cadences that reference women using derogatory slurs, describe sexual conquest in graphic or non-consensual terms, objectify women or campus auxiliaries, or distort official sweetheart songs into sexually explicit material.

Common motifs: Use of slurs ("hoes," "bitches," "freaks") directed at women or sorority members; chants bragging about intoxicated sexual encounters; unofficial parody lyrics grafted onto the Sweetheart Song or other official fraternal music; chants framing women attending fraternity events as sexual objects rather than guests.

Why this matters beyond policy: These cadences harm real people. Women who attend fraternity events, auxiliary members, sorority women on campus — they hear these chants. They document them. They report them. And when they do, the chapter and the individual brothers face institutional, fraternal, and legal consequences. There is no version of this behavior that is consistent with being a Kappa man.

Policy basis: Any call, chant, or modified song containing sexually explicit language, references to non-consensual acts, or derogatory characterization of women or campus auxiliaries is prohibited conduct unbecoming.

Category 3 — Violence, Hazing & Illegal Intake Nostalgia

Chants that romanticize physical hazing, reference paddles or other instruments of physical coercion, frame underground intake rituals as a rite of passage, or portray violence against prospective members as "earning" membership.

Common motifs: "Take that wood" — and variations referencing paddles as punishment instruments; chants framing line servitude or physical conditioning under duress as legitimate; cadences that glorify what happened in underground intake processes as the "real" path to membership; references to "earning your letters" through physical or psychological abuse.

Why this is particularly dangerous: These cadences do double harm. First, they romanticize illegal activity — hazing is a crime in Texas and in virtually every U.S. jurisdiction. Second, they create pressure on new members who may believe the mythology that "real Kappas" went through something physical. That mythology is false, it is prohibited, and it has resulted in deaths, lawsuits, criminal convictions, and charter revocations across Black Greek-letter organizations. DLA does not have that history. We are not building that history.

Policy basis: Any cadence referencing paddles, beatings, physical conditioning under duress, line obedience, or illegal intake processes is explicitly prohibited and constitutes conduct subject to immediate disciplinary action.

Category 4 — Disparagement of Other Greek Organizations

Chants, stroll theatrics, or call-and-response sequences designed to mock, degrade, or demean fellow NPHC organizations, their founders, their symbols, or their members.

Common motifs: Corrupting the official calls or names of other Divine Nine organizations with derogatory rhymes or slurs; making insulting sexual claims about peer sororities or fraternities; chants aimed at provoking inter-organizational conflict during step shows, yard strolls, or probate ceremonies; mocking the sacred traditions or founding stories of peer organizations.

The NPHC covenant: Kappa Alpha Psi is a founding member of the National Pan-Hellenic Council. The nine NPHC organizations have a shared covenant of mutual respect. Disparaging another Divine Nine organization in public — especially at an event where multiple organizations are present — violates that covenant, embarrasses the fraternity, and can trigger formal complaints through the NPHC structure.

Policy basis: Disparagement of other Greek-letter organizations, including derogatory wordplay on organizational names, mocking of sacred symbols, or chants aimed at inciting inter-organizational conflict, is prohibited conduct.

Category 5 — Gang Culture & Street Violence Emulation

Cadences that mimic local street gang vernacular, threaten violence against non-members or rival groups, associate the fraternity's colors and call with territorial gang concepts, or reference firearms and weapons in a threatening context.

Common motifs: Chants warning rivals they will be "stomped," "jumped," or "put in the dirt"; associating Crimson and Cream with neighborhood territorial claims; incorporating gang hand signs or phrases into stroll performances; chants that frame the fraternity as a protection racket or street organization.

Policy basis: Chants, calls, or stroll theatrics that emulate street gang rhetoric, threaten physical violence, or reference firearms and weapons are prohibited and may constitute criminal threatening depending on context and jurisdiction.

1. The Myth vs. Reality of Underground Intake

In collegiate Greek-letter culture, informal underground traditions often attempt to validate themselves through pseudo-historical authority. A central challenge in risk management is confronting the belief that official, national intake processes produce “paper” members — whereas unsanctioned, underground processes produce “real” or “made” members.

This false mythology is not just culturally harmful. It is legally dangerous. The table below confronts each claim directly:

Concept The Underground Lore Organizational & Legal Reality
“Getting Made” Framed as an exclusive rite of passage involving physical duress, servitude, or psychological endurance. Unauthorized, illegal hazing that carries state and federal criminal liability, civil liability, and permanent expulsion from the Fraternity.
“Paper” vs. “Real” Derogatory hierarchy suggesting members initiated through official national protocol have not “earned” their letters. Fractures chapter cohesion, directly violates national fraternal constitutions, and devalues the legitimate, ritualistic process established by the Founders.
“True Nupe” Rhetoric Used by rogue underground factions to claim an elevated, purist status over brothers who followed official protocols. A false binary designed to justify hazing, preserve underground gatekeeping, and exert coercive control over aspirants.
⚠ Zero Tolerance

There is no legitimate version of underground intake. There is no “real” way that involves hazing. Any brother who perpetuates these myths — in conversation, in group chats, or in chapter spaces — is actively undermining the Fraternity’s legal standing and his own.

2. Historical Subversion: Co-opting Identity & Underground Factions

A common tactic among rogue groups is co-opting historical campus groups or pop-culture identities to manufacture a false pedigree for illicit pledge lines.

Historical Distortion — The “Strollers” at Ohio State (1919)

Early Black collegiate social clubs, such as the Strollers organization established around 1919 at The Ohio State University, existed in an era of intense racial segregation prior to the formal chartering of many Black Greek-letter chapters on predominantly white campuses. These early groups served critical historical roles in social cohesion and mutual support during the pioneering days of Black higher education.

Modern underground factions frequently co-opt these historical names to manufacture a false pedigree for illicit pledge lines — borrowing the credibility of the civil rights era to justify 21st-century hazing. This is historical distortion, not heritage preservation.

Pop-Culture & Secret Coteries — “Justice League” and Similar Monikers

Underground factions frequently adopt comic book, military, or fraternal monikers — such as “Justice League” — to brand their unofficial pledge lines. These groups operate outside chapter oversight, run unauthorized off-campus sessions, and introduce hazing rituals completely detached from national ritualistic work.

The use of branded names creates a sense of identity and exclusivity that makes participants feel they are part of something special — when in reality they are participating in something illegal, and exposing themselves and the chapter to serious legal liability.

3. Prohibited Indicators & Red-Flag Behaviors

Chapter leadership and risk management officers must actively monitor and intervene when any of the following indicators emerge. These are not gray areas — they are bright-line violations:

01

Linguistic Gatekeeping

Utilizing phrases such as “made,” “paper,” “over the burning sands,” or referencing unsanctioned line factions in person, in chapter meetings, or across chapter group chats. Language shapes culture — and this language builds the wrong one.

02

Shadow Sessions

Organizing off-campus meetings, late-night study sessions, workout drills, or unapproved gatherings of candidates outside authorized membership orientation hours. If it’s not on the official schedule, it should not be happening.

03

Subversive Apparel & Branding

Producing, distributing, or wearing custom jackets, jerseys, or shirts bearing unapproved slogans, faction names, or unofficial line designations. Trademark use is governed by IHQ — no chapter, line, or faction has authority to create parallel branding.

04

Servitude & Chores

The following standards govern all members, candidates, and advisors. This language is structured for direct inclusion in chapter bylaws, risk management agreements, and candidate handbooks.

Sole Authority — The Only Recognized Intake Method

The only recognized method for obtaining membership in the Fraternity is the strict execution of the official Membership Intake Process (MIP) as authorized by National Headquarters and overseen by certified alumni advisors. Prohibition of Factionalism & Underground Lore

No member or candidate shall form, promote, or participate in any unauthorized auxiliary, society, or underground faction — including, but not limited to, groups designating themselves as “Strollers,” “Justice League,” “True Nupe,” or similar informal designations. Membership in, promotion of, or association with such factions is sanctionable conduct.

Elimination of Informal Membership Hierarchy

The creation of informal membership tiers — including referring to any initiated brother as “paper” or claiming individuals must be “made” through unsanctioned physical or psychological testing — is classified as conduct unbecoming a member and constitutes grounds for disciplinary action up to and including permanent expulsion.

Mandatory Reporting & Liability

Any member who has knowledge of, participates in, or conceals parallel underground sessions is subject to:

  • Referral to the National Judicial Board

The Founding — March 1, 1997

The Denton-Lewisville (TX) Alumni Chapter began as the Denton (TX) Alumni Chapter of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc. It was chartered on March 1, 1997, at the Ridgecrest Apartment Clubhouse in Denton, Texas — founded by brothers who were predominantly Zeta Upsilon Chapter alumni from the University of North Texas.

The chartering ceremony was presided over by Dwayne M. Murray, Esq., then serving as Southwest Provincial Polemarch. Murray would go on to become the 31st Grand Polemarch of Kappa Alpha Psi — a connection that links DLA's founding directly to the highest levels of fraternity leadership.

The Southwest Province formally endorsed the chapter's petition in November 1996, setting the stage for the March 1997 chartering ceremony.
